## Abstract

Choosing a checkpoint, layer, threshold, or hyperparameter on data that is later used to report performance biases the reported metric, and every bias we measure is optimistic. **We show this selection-induced optimism is real and code-verified in hidden-state hallucination-detection pipelines, and that the hard part is not detecting it but measuring its size: severity is a property of a (mechanism, metric, *control*) triple, not of a mechanism alone.**

We identify five structurally distinct selection mechanisms across two published pipelines and our own – three verified directly against pinned third-party source code – and measure each against its own matched control (§4). Four are small on the AUROC scale under their own distinct, non-interchangeable controls (Table~, §3); one – our own original headline estimate for a per-fold checkpoint-selection harness – does not survive being corrected for its own construction confounds at all, a finding we report first because it demonstrates the measurement problem on our own work before anyone else's. The fifth mechanism sits on a different scale entirely, an order of magnitude larger, consistent with but not an independent confirmation of an existing unverified estimate for it in the literature. Full per-mechanism estimates and what does and does not survive correction are in §4.

**What governs severity, and what remains open.** Within a single controlled harness, severity is governed jointly by candidate count and the pipeline's operating point, with the operating point dominant; the candidate-count relationship is scoped specifically to checkpoint-along-a-trajectory selection and, at adequate power, does not reproduce for independently-seeded exchangeable candidates such as layers or thresholds (§5.3). A matched comparison across all five mechanisms shows a further factor beyond these two covariates is not absorbed by them, but this design cannot yet separate whether that factor is mechanism identity itself or the unequal harness maturity correlated with it (§5.9). **We do not rank the three axes on this evidence** – what it licenses is only a negative conclusion, that severity is not governed by candidate count and operating point to the exclusion of mechanism (§5.2, §5.9).

Further robustness checks – an estimator-calibration control, a second real-feature harness, and a second model family – are consistent with this account and do not change the headline severities (§5, Appendix~). The severity surface is an empirical fit, not a bound, and whether these estimates transfer to real hidden states beyond the checks already run is untested. We release a code-verified five-mechanism taxonomy, this severity relationship, a label-free calibration guardrail, and an extension of the Kapoor & Narayanan/REFORMS leakage checklists – together with a negative result of our own: a regex scanner built to automate the checklist yields zero true positives over 7 repositories and misses both mechanisms it was built to catch (§6).

The **Organization** paragraph at the end of this section maps every section in full; readers who want the section-by-section route before the narrative below can skip to it now.

Detecting hallucination by probing an LLM's internal hidden states is one of the most active subfields in LLM reliability research: a linear probe is cheap to train and needs no additional model calls, and reported AUROCs are often striking – 0.90 or higher, sometimes a perfect 1.000. This is also a setting with few samples and many dimensions (hundreds of samples, thousands of hidden dimensions), where high reported numbers should invite scrutiny of the evaluation protocol that produced them. **This paper's claim is that scrutinizing that protocol is harder than it looks, because the size of the resulting bias depends on exactly which control a reported number is compared against, not on which selection mechanism produced it.** A single Mechanism-3 cell alone spans 1.45x across two admissible controls (§2, §4.3) – severity is a property of a (mechanism, metric, *control*) triple, not of a mechanism alone, and that measurement problem, not the leakage itself, is this paper's central contribution.

This paper began when we found and fixed a nested-cross-validation leakage bug in our own hidden-state hallucination detector (HaRP), an accident that raised a broader question: is this an isolated mistake, or a structural hazard the wider literature shares? We find five *distinct* mechanisms by which evaluation labels can leak into a reported metric, verify three of them directly against pinned commits of two externally published pipelines, and measure each against a matched control (§4). Most are small on the AUROC scale; one is not, and one – this paper's own original headline estimate for a per-fold checkpoint-selection harness – does not survive being corrected for its own construction asymmetries at all, a result we report prominently precisely because it demonstrates the measurement problem on our own work first (§4.3). §5 then asks what governs severity within a controlled harness (candidate count and operating point, jointly, with a scope limit that keeps this an empirical regularity rather than a law) and, in §5.9, whether mechanism identity matters beyond those two covariates – it does, though this comparison cannot yet say whether that reflects identity itself or unequal harness maturity.

## 2. Related Work

**The definition we use.** *Leakage is a change in the expectation of a reported metric attributable to evaluation-target information entering the estimation process.* Three properties of this definition are load-bearing. First, the change is *signed*. It is about a change in an *expectation*, and we do not claim to have observed a leak whose expected effect is deflationary: every mechanism measured here comes out positive in expectation. What we do observe is that finite realizations of these contrasts take either sign — the anisotropic sweep's capacity-384 CLEAN_MATCHED-minus-PLACEBO cell (§4.3) is negative — and, more to the point, that nothing in the mechanism guarantees the sign a priori: which of two selection procedures wins on a given draw is an empirical question. A definition that builds "inflates" in makes that question unaskable and would license reading any negative measurement as measurement error. Second, the change is *metric-relative*: the same leak can move one reported metric while leaving another provably invariant — Mechanism 5 (§4.5) selects a decision threshold on the test labels, which inflates every threshold-dependent metric the same pipeline reports while having exactly zero effect on its AUROC, by construction rather than by measurement. **Third, the change is *control-relative* as well.** Severity is not a property of a (mechanism, metric) *pair* but of a (mechanism, metric, control) *triple*: a severity number is the difference between the leaky procedure and *some* counterfactual, and defensible counterfactuals disagree. On one Mechanism-3 cell this paper's own admissible control family spans 1.45x, and a control it disqualifies on independent grounds would make that 5.9x — §4.3 gives the numbers and the disqualifying argument, since they belong with the experiment rather than with the definition. Every severity number here is therefore reported as a signed change in a named metric *against a named control*, with §4.3 reporting the spread across the control family rather than only one member of it.

**The pipelines this paper audits: hidden-state hallucination probing.** The immediate subfield trains a light classifier on a frozen LLM's internal activations to predict whether a generation is hallucinated, following Kadavath et al. (2022)'s finding that models carry usable self-knowledge: truth-related structure is linearly decodable from hidden states (Azaria & Mitchell 2023; Burns et al. 2023, without supervision; Chen et al. 2024, via the covariance spectrum), a signal several methods approximate at different granularities – semantic entropy over sampled generations (Farquhar et al. 2024) or from a single generation's hidden states (Kossen et al. 2024), an unsupervised subspace (Du et al. 2024), specific answer tokens (Orgad et al. 2024), or entity-level attribution in long-form output (Obeso et al. 2025). This is the literature whose evaluation protocol we audit, and seven of its repositories are vendored here — five of them scanned in §6.2 as presumed-clean pipelines, including Du et al.'s, whose layer and threshold selection reading the code shows to be correct. We take no position on any of these methods' detection quality; our concern is upstream of it, that the protocol producing the number can inflate it regardless of which representation is used. Leakage in this general sense is well documented in data mining (Kaufman et al. 2012) and in model-selection statistics: cross-validation-based hyperparameter or feature selection is optimistic unless nested inside the outer evaluation loop (Varma & Simon 2006).

**Adaptive data analysis and post-selection inference.** Mechanisms 4 and 5 — select a layer, or a decision threshold, on the test set and then report at that choice — are the canonical setting of the adaptive data analysis literature: a held-out set queried repeatedly, with each query permitted to depend on the answers to earlier ones. Dwork et al. (2015a, 2015b) show that naive reuse of a holdout under K adaptive queries degrades validity at a rate governed by K and the holdout size, and give mechanisms (the reusable holdout; the Ladder of Blum & Hardt 2015) that restore it. Post-selection inference (Berk et al. 2013; Taylor & Tibshirani 2015) attacks the same problem from the other end, constructing intervals valid *after* a data-dependent model choice. This literature bears on this paper in two specific ways, and we flag both rather than claim the connection is novel. First, it is the natural home of the bound §7's Limitation (2a) names as open: its results scale like sqrt(log K/n) for K adaptive queries against a holdout of size n, a principled reason to expect a severity family *concave in* ln K — and §5.3 measures exactly such a family, without being able to identify its functional form (Appendix~). Second, its guarantees are worst-case over adversarial query sequences, while the pipelines audited here issue one short, benign, non-adaptive sweep; the severities we measure are correspondingly hundredths of an AUROC rather than the collapse the worst case permits, and neither figure contradicts the other. **The canonical treatment of Mechanism 2 is Cawley & Talbot (2010)**, who establish that over-fitting the model-selection criterion itself induces a selection bias in the reported performance estimate, that the bias grows with the number of candidates compared, and that unbiased evaluation requires the selection to be nested inside the outer resampling loop. That is precisely Mechanism 2's failure mode, and this paper claims no novelty for the mechanism itself: **Mechanism 2 is a specific instance of a known failure mode.** What is added here is narrower — the selected object is a *layer index of a frozen pretrained network*, and in this literature that index is reported not only as a performance figure but as a substantive claim about *where* in the network the signal is decodable, so the selection bias contaminates a localization claim, not only a number. Case Study 2 measures this directly and finds the argmax layer itself moves with the selection rule (L11 under train-split CV, L21 under all-data AUROC). Case Studies 2 and 4 are otherwise direct instances of the general phenomenon in hidden-state probing; Case Studies 1 and 3 are more specific to the two-stage feature-extraction-then-classification structure common in this literature.

**Existing leakage taxonomies and checklists.** The closest prior art is Kapoor & Narayanan (2023), who survey leakage across 17 scientific fields and 294 papers and propose an 8-type taxonomy, and REFORMS (Kapoor et al. 2024), a 32-item consensus checklist for ML-based science whose data-leakage module asks three questions. Mechanisms 4 and 5 are direct cases of Kapoor & Narayanan's **L1.3** — the test set is reused for a modeling decision and then for the reported metric; Mechanism 1 is an instance of L1.2, generalized from a preprocessing statistic to an entire fitted feature-extraction model; Mechanism 3 is a narrower-bandwidth variant of the same pattern; and all three REFORMS leakage questions are subsumed by the taxonomy here. Mechanism 2 (CV-based layer/hyperparameter selection optimism) maps cleanly onto neither instrument, because both treat train/test separation and feature legitimacy as the leakage surface rather than iterative model selection against a validation signal. The claim we draw from this is only about the *taxonomies*, not about the mechanism: a well-established model-selection failure mode is absent from the two leakage checklists this literature is most likely to reach for. It is not a claim that the failure mode is new; the model-selection literature has described it for fifteen years. The NeurIPS ML Reproducibility Checklist (Pineau et al. 2021) is reporting-practice-oriented rather than leakage-specific and would not, on its own, have caught any of the sixteen issues Appendix~ documents in this paper's own instrument: the two kinds of checklist are complementary, not interchangeable. The item-by-item comparison against all three instruments is in `EXTENDED_TECHNICAL_DETAIL.md` in the supplementary material.

**Calibration and benchmark-construction critiques.** A large, separate literature asks whether LLM confidence is calibrated to correctness; that work treats the evaluation protocol as given, whereas our contribution sits upstream of it — an inflated AUROC corrupts any downstream calibration analysis built on it. Closer to our concern, PARALLAX (arXiv 2605.17028) audits 22 detection methods across 12 models and 6 corpora and finds four of six corpora leak the ground-truth answer directly into the input prompt, with most baselines falling to near chance once controlled for — independent, larger-scale evidence that the field's reported AUROCs are frequently protocol or construction artifacts, though its mechanism (benchmark construction) is distinct from all five documented here, each of which concerns a downstream selection step. Separately, surface-form correctness metrics (ROUGE-L, exact match) are known to diverge from LLM-judge or human factual-correctness judgments: a related but distinct label-quality concern this paper does not address. Fixing the leakage patterns we identify does not, by itself, address label-quality or benchmark-construction problems in the underlying dataset.

**Pretraining-data contamination.** A separate 2023–2026 thread asks whether a benchmark's answers appeared in an LLM's pretraining corpus (Shi et al. 2024; Golchin & Surdeanu 2024; Sainz et al. 2023) — a different failure mode from ours: contamination concerns what the model has already seen, not whether a downstream selection step used test-set signal after the model was already fixed. The two are complementary and, in principle, additive; we do not audit our four case studies' benchmarks for contamination.

To our knowledge, no prior paper both (a) names and distinguishes multiple, structurally different label-leakage mechanisms specific to hidden-state probing pipelines, and (b) quantifies their relative severity via controlled reconstruction rather than assuming all leakage is equally damaging.

## 3. Method: How These Case Studies Were Selected, Graded, and Measured

Two case studies (§4.1–4.2) come from our own prior work, where we had full access to code, data and commit history. The external ones (§4.3–4.4) required three things: a real, runnable public code repository, verified directly via the repository host rather than taken on faith from the paper's text; a reported AUROC in the range that warrants scrutiny (0.90 or higher); and an evaluation protocol whose leakage risk we could assess by reading the actual training/evaluation code. We did not limit the search to K-fold setups, since one candidate paper's bug (§4.4) lives in a single train/val/test split with no K-fold structure at all — "avoid K-fold" is not itself a fix for this family of hazards. Neither HaRP nor GUARDIAN was run through the automated scanner of §6, since both were built and their leaks discovered before that scanner existed.

When this paper says severities "cluster" between +0.0011 and +0.0255, it describes the numerical range the *measured* quantities occupy, taking each estimate against the control it was reported with — explicitly *not* a claim that they are interchangeable measurements of one underlying scale. Mechanism 5's F1 and accuracy gaps are on a different metric entirely and are never placed in that band, and its exact zero on AUROC is an algebraic property of a threshold-free metric rather than a measurement. Because §5 shows the gap falls to the floor across the operating-point range, a Delta_real and a Delta_synth measured at different operating points are not comparable in magnitude either. The *achieved* operating point is not identical across cells even when the target is: it varies across capacities (LEAKY 0.7478–0.7633 in §4.3's primary table) and, at the same target, across generative processes (the anisotropic sweep lands at 0.672–0.681 where the isotropic one lands at 0.753–0.765), which is why §4.3 declines the synthetic-versus-real magnitude comparison.

**Statistical decision rule, stated once.** Most severity estimates here are plain differences between two arms' AUROCs (Case Studies 2–3, §5.2–§5.3's severity-surface axes, and Mechanism 5). These are unconstrained quantities — free to be positive, negative or zero — and for them BCa is this paper's default interval method: where it disagrees with Wilcoxon or a Holm-adjusted family-wise threshold, the BCa 95% interval is the decision criterion (an effect is established when it excludes zero), and the other two are reported alongside as diagnostic context rather than as an alternative rule. Case Study 4 is the one place this default is suspended, for a stated reason. Its two candidate estimators are each built from a max over seed rotations or bootstrap resamples minus a mean — a maximum is never less than the mean it is compared against, which is what makes both provably non-negative for *any* input data (Theorems~ and~) — a hard boundary constraint an ordinary difference of AUROCs does not share. A bootstrap resample of a statistic with that structure reproduces the same one-sided bias in every resample, which is exactly the condition under which BCa's bias-and-acceleration correction cannot restore coverage: simulation against a known ground truth confirms it, at 0% empirical coverage of the true bias for both bootstrap variants and 37–77% for the retired rotation estimator, against a nominal 95%. §4.4 therefore never uses a BCa interval as evidence.

**What follows if a reader prefers the stricter, family-wise standard instead.** We state this consequence here, upfront, rather than only in Limitations (§7), because it changes how every BCa-established claim in §4 should be weighed. This paper's pre-committed rule is the unadjusted per-cell BCa interval, not a family-wise-corrected one. Where we additionally compute Holm-Bonferroni across cells tested together (Table~), it eliminates the marginal finding in every family we checked except one: Table~'s four real-feature/fidelity-extension cells all lose significance under Holm across that family (p_Holm>=0.68, §4.3.3), and the primary synthetic sweep's one nominally Holm-surviving capacity does not survive once the factorial's corrected control replaces the shipped one (§4.3.2). The single exception is the anisotropic-covariance sweep, whose two capacity-128 cells remain significant when pooled with the other seven synthetic tests into one Holm family (§4.3.4). A reader applying family-wise control across this paper's full set of reported cells should therefore expect essentially no marginal positive finding to survive outside Mechanism 5's exact-zero-by-construction AUROC result (whose F1/accuracy inflation is a single large, non-marginal effect, p<5x10^-8, not part of any multi-cell family) and that one anisotropic-sweep exception. ### 4.1 Case Study 1: full-dataset fit-then-score leakage (HaRP) — mechanism only, number not re-verifiable from this artifact

[See `draft/worked_examples.md`, Case Study 1, for full detail.] **The numbers here are reported from our own prior work and are not reproducible from the materials submitted with this paper** (§3). No script, log or result file supporting them is included. They are stated for completeness, because this bug is what started this paper and because the mechanism is worth naming; they are excluded from the abstract's severity range and from every cross-mechanism comparison here.

HaRP's Group-C representation feature `probe_conf` came from a logistic-regression probe fit on all 700 samples, which then scored those same 700 samples; only afterward did this feature enter a separate outer 5-fold cross-validation loop for the downstream failure estimator. This is nested-CV leakage in its cleanest form: the "held-out" fold's feature was manufactured by a model that had already seen every label, including that fold's own. **Quantified effect.** A+B+C AUROC under 5-fold CV: 0.9620 (leaked) against 0.7714 (fixed, out-of-fold) — a **+0.1906** inflation. `probe_conf` alone: in-sample AUROC 1.0000 against a proper out-of-fold AUROC of 0.7573, independently cross-checked against a separately-obtained cross-validated AUROC of 0.7583 (agreement within 0.001). **Fix:** refit both the probe and the class centroids per fold, using only that fold's training indices, scoring only the held-out indices.

**A first code-verified reconstruction of the general mechanism (`code/86`), not of HaRP's own number.** Every other mechanism in this paper has a matched-control synthetic estimate; Mechanism 1 did not, so its absence from the abstract's four-estimate severity band could be read either as "too small to matter" or "not yet measured." `code/86` builds the same kind of isotropic-Gaussian synthetic reconstruction §5 already uses for Mechanisms 3 and beyond, calibrated to a Bayes-optimal AUROC_0 via the same binormal identity: a single `LogisticRegression` probe is fit once on *all* n=200 samples (d=60 features) and scored in-sample (LEAKY), against a 5-fold out-of-fold reconstruction refitting the identical probe per fold (CLEAN_MATCHED), n=100 seeds per cell, BCa 95% intervals via the same methodology as every other severity number in this paper. At AUROC_0in 0.70,0.80,0.95 the gaps are +0.2914 [+0.2835,+0.2994], +0.2495 [+0.2421,+0.2569] and +0.1272 [+0.1210,+0.1336] respectively — all three BCa-established, all Wilcoxon p<10^-17, 100/100 seeds positive at every cell. **These are tenths of an AUROC, not hundredths, and an order of magnitude above every Mechanism 2–4 estimate in Table~.** The result is not a recomputation of HaRP's +0.19 — this harness does not port HaRP's architecture, its A+B+C feature composition or its 4096-dimensional hidden states, and its own achieved CLEAN_MATCHED operating point falls below its AUROC_0 target because d=60 is large relative to the sim160 training samples per fold, so a finite-sample logistic regression underperforms the Bayes rule it is calibrated against (a property of this reconstruction, not evidence about HaRP's). What it does establish is that the *general* mechanism — a probe fit on the full dataset and reused as a downstream feature — produces severities on a different scale from the four estimates this paper otherwise pools in its abstract. Both this estimate and HaRP's own unverified +0.19 occupy the tenths-of-AUROC range rather than the hundredths Mechanisms 2–4 occupy; that is not independent corroboration of HaRP's specific figure, which comes from an architecturally unrelated harness (HaRP's 4096-dimensional A+B+C feature composition versus this reconstruction's d=60 synthetic one) and remains unverifiable from this artifact. What it does support is that Mechanism 1 should be read as plausibly the most severe of the five, not merely as excluded for lack of code – though a real-feature check of this same mechanism, immediately below, finds a gap an order of magnitude smaller at a higher operating point, so this reading should not be taken as settled. §5.1, §7 and the abstract are scoped accordingly.

**A real-feature analog, on the same cached Mistral-7B/HaluEval features Mechanism 3 uses.** Unlike Mechanisms 3–5, Mechanism 1's severity above is measured only in a synthetic harness. `code/94` closes that gap using `results/real_features_mistral7b_halueval.npz` directly — no new GPU extraction, only a new probe-fitting comparison on already-extracted features: fit one `LogisticRegression` on all 400 samples (d=414), score those same 400 samples (LEAKY), against a 5-fold out-of-fold refit (CLEAN_MATCHED). Standardized, default C=1: LEAKY reaches AUROC 1.0000 (exact in-sample separation, unsurprising with d>n/2) against an out-of-fold AUROC of 0.9889, a gap of +0.0111 (stable to fold assignment: BCa interval [+0.0107,+0.0117] over 40 fold-seed draws). A regularization sweep (Cin0.01,0.1,1,10) moves the gap only from +0.0098 to +0.0113 — not a regularization artifact — and an unstandardized version (raw feature magnitudes up to sim1980, which makes `lbfgs` converge slowly and is reported with a smaller fold-seed budget accordingly) gives +0.0258, still the same order of magnitude. **This real-feature gap is roughly an order of magnitude smaller than the synthetic reconstruction's +0.13 to +0.29** — more than Mechanism 3's real-feature gaps shrink at the fold-matched control (65–87% retained), because the real-feature analog and the synthetic reconstruction disagree by more than an order of magnitude in the same direction they disagree for the CLEAN_MATCHED operating point itself (real OOF AUROC 0.989, far above any of `code/86`'s calibrated AUROC_0 targets). We read this as consistent with §5.3's operating-point account — Mechanism 1's synthetic harness runs at AUROC_0in0.70,0.80,0.95, well below this real-feature setting's ~0.99 achieved operating point, where severity elsewhere in this paper shrinks sharply — rather than as evidence the synthetic reconstruction is wrong; the two are not measured at a matched operating point, so a direct magnitude comparison is exactly the kind §4.3 and §5.4 already decline to make for Mechanism 3, and we decline it here for the same reason. What this does establish, honestly and against this paper's own preceding paragraph: on the one real feature set this mechanism has been checked against, the gap is closer to Mechanisms 2–4's scale than to the tenths-of-an-AUROC scale the synthetic reconstruction reports, so a reader should weight the +0.13-to-+0.29 figure as an upper-bound-shaped estimate at a low operating point rather than as this mechanism's severity at any operating point.

### 4.2 Case Study 2: CV-based layer-selection optimism (GUARDIAN) — Delta_sel=+0.0255 AUROC after correction, original 18.8-point headline retracted

[See `draft/worked_examples.md`, Case Study 2, for full detail.]

GUARDIAN selects its detection layer — L11 of 32, on Mistral-7B — by argmax of a 5-fold cross-validated AUROC across all layers, then reports that same CV number as the model's performance. This is the failure mode Cawley & Talbot (2010) analyze, and we claim no novelty for the mechanism; their account already predicts both the sign of the bias and its growth with the number of candidates compared, 32 here. Two things are added, and only these two. First, the selected object is a layer index of a frozen pretrained network, which this literature reports as a scientific finding about *where* hallucination is decodable; the bias therefore contaminates a localization claim as well as a performance number, and the argmax layer is unstable under a change of selection rule — argmax train-split CV picks L11, argmax all-data AUROC picks L21 (0.7829; `code/61`). Second, we quantify the bias in situ on a published pipeline at its own operating point.

**The reported effect, and what it actually was.** CV-selection AUROC at L11: 0.804. True held-out AUROC at L11 (trained on the first 400 samples, tested on a separate, never-selection-touched 300): **0.616** — an 18.8-point gap. **That gap does not reflect selection optimism at all.** GUARDIAN's raw cache holds the complete (700, 32, 4096) representations, so `code/48` repeats the identical comparison at all 32 layers under GUARDIAN's own sequential split: the mean optimism gap is +0.192 (SD 0.053), indistinguishable from L11's own +0.188, with 18 of the other 31 layers showing a *larger* gap than L11, and the per-layer gap rising close to monotonically with depth from 0.067 at L0 to 0.261 at L31 — a property of *where in the network you probe*, not of the selection. The cause is that `H[:400]`/`H[400:]` is a fixed slice of whatever order the dataset happens to store samples in, not a random partition: `code/61` trains a logistic probe on the raw hidden states to predict half-membership and reaches an out-of-fold AUROC of 0.7885 at its best layer (L25), above 0.70 at 25 of the 32 layers, mean 0.7259. **A simpler and fully verifiable version of the same confound sits in the shipped replay artifact and we had not reported it:** the two halves differ in *label base rate* by 8.0 percentage points — positive rate 0.7300 (n=400) against 0.8100 (n=300) — while the randomized comparison reps are stratified and match to within 0.17 points. An 8-point base-rate difference is by itself enough to make the halves separable and their AUROCs non-comparable, by a shorter route than a general population difference. It is not the whole confound, though: subsampling both halves to a common positive rate of 0.73 and re-running over 20 draws leaves Delta_sel at -0.0050 (SD 0.0058) against -0.0038 unmatched, with L11 selected in every draw. The sequential split is unusable for this estimate either way, which is why the corrected result uses randomized stratified splits. **Fix:** after CV-based selection, evaluate exactly once more on a disjoint held-out set that played no role in the selection.

**What isolates selection optimism specifically** is the *selection-specific component* Delta_sel (Table~), evaluated at whichever layer each split's own CV-argmax actually selects. Recomputed under randomized stratified 400/300 splits it gives Delta_sel=+0.0255 (SD 0.0186; 50 reps; the selected layer spans 14 distinct values from L5 to L23, and GUARDIAN's own L11 is selected in only 2 of the 50). The *general* CV-vs-held-out gap averaged across all 32 layers, by contrast, is +0.192 under the sequential split but only -0.0023 (SD 0.0541, t=-0.30, p=0.76) under the same randomized splits — indistinguishable from zero. The retracted +0.188 was 7x larger, but the two are not the same estimand and we do not treat that ratio as meaningful: +0.188 is a raw CV-vs-held-out gap and Delta_sel is the selection-specific component of one, so the comparison shows how much of the original headline was *not* selection optimism, not that selection optimism shrank sevenfold.

\textbf{Delta_sel has a mechanical null, not a zero one, and that is what makes +0.0255 informative.} Delta_sel is positive in expectation even under a pure-noise null, since l^star=argmax_lcv_l while gap_l=cv_l-ho_l contains the same +cv_l term, so testing H_0:Delta_sel=0 is not the informative test: the reference is what the same estimator returns when the layer ordering carries no *transferable* information. `code/64` computes it from the shipped 1 MB replay artifact by permuting which layer's held-out AUROC is paired with which layer's CV AUROC — leaving both marginal vectors, the argmax rule and the layer count untouched, and removing only the correspondence — 20,000 draws, independently per rep per draw. **The null mean is +0.0429** (SD 0.0043, 95% [+0.0350,+0.0517]), and the observed +0.0255 lies *below that entire interval* (P(null<=observed)=0.0000). Because mathbbE_pi[ho_pi(l)]=overlineho, the null's mean has a closed form and the statistic decomposes exactly: Delta_sel=A-B, where A=cv_l^star-overlinecv is the winner's curse on the selection criterion (the null's own mean; +0.042865, matching the simulated +0.042936 to 7x10^-5) and B=ho_l^star-overlineho is how much better than an average layer the CV-selected layer actually is *on data that played no part in selecting it*. Here B=+0.0173 (SD 0.0135 over the 50 reps, t=9.07, p=4.6x10^-12, negative in only 7). **CV-argmax layer selection therefore carries real, transferable signal, and that signal cancels 40.4% of the raw winner's curse.** The right reading of +0.0255 is not "selection optimism is significantly positive" — an unsurprising claim against a null this paper calls false — but "40% of what argmax-over-noise would cost is bought back by the selected layer genuinely being better, and +0.0255 is the residual optimism a practitioner still pays." Instability of the argmax and informativeness of the argmax are both true here, and neither alone describes the situation.

**What that p-value does and does not mean.** All 50 reps resample splits of the *same* 700 samples, so the SD is split-to-split variability conditional on one dataset, not sampling variability across independent draws, and the one-sample t-test's i.i.d. assumption is violated. Read its p as a within-dataset robustness statistic — "not an artifact of which particular split we took" — and *not* as evidence the effect generalizes beyond GUARDIAN's own 700-sample TruthfulQA sample; §4.4's estimate, drawn from a fully crossed 3x4x2 design of 24 published result cells, is the closest thing here to a replicated version of the same claim, though those 24 cells are not independent either. **A robustness check on the probe configuration.** The layer probe is `LogisticRegression` at d=4096, n=400, default C=1.0, on unscaled raw hidden states — reported as primary because it is what GUARDIAN itself ran — which could plausibly be so heavily regularized that both estimates are dominated by regularization rather than fit. It is not: the largest `n_iter_` across the 9,600 probe fits behind the primary result is 125, with zero `ConvergenceWarning`s, and adding `StandardScaler` while sweeping C over five decades leaves both conclusions intact — with the unscaled configuration, if anything, the mildly conservative one. The full 32-value per-layer gap vector, the per-rep Delta_sel quantiles and the C-sweep values are in `EXTENDED_TECHNICAL_DETAIL.md` in the supplementary material.

### 4.3 Case Study 3: per-fold checkpoint-selection leakage (MultiHaluDet) — a factorial that decomposes a false-positive severity estimate, and Delta_real=+0.0060 to +0.0159 on real features once the control is fold-matched

[See `draft/case_study_multihaludet.md` for full code excerpts. Appendix~ documents the correction history behind this section's numbers; §4.3 states the result directly and nothing in the appendix is required to verify it.]

MultiHaluDet (arXiv 2605.24919; repo `github.com/alvi-uiu/MultiHaluDet`, pinned at commit `c7597518`) reports up to 98.55% AUROC detecting hallucination from Mistral-7B/LLaMA-2-7B hidden states. Its training code (`run_pipeline.py::stage_2_3_train_oof`, `src/training/trainer.py::train_deep_model_fold`) selects each inner fold's kept checkpoint by argmax validation AUROC on that same fold, then stores that fold's features as "out-of-fold" for a downstream meta-learner. The checkpoint choice is a function of the validation fold's own labels; unlike Case Study 1, only the *checkpoint selection*, not gradient descent itself, sees those labels. The mechanism is a code-verified property of the audited repository, not an inference from our harness.

**Three conditions, shared by every harness in this subsection.** **LEAKY** chooses the checkpoint on the reused fold. **CLEAN_MATCHED** chooses it on a disjoint carve-out, then retrains from scratch on the full training budget for the resulting epoch count, using the same seed as LEAKY — isolating fold reuse from data budget. **PLACEBO** chooses it on permuted labels: zero *selection* signal, but real training signal, so it lands well above chance rather than at 0.5. Severity is LEAKY minus CLEAN_MATCHED, while CLEAN_MATCHED minus PLACEBO is what honest checkpoint selection is worth; this subsection reports both throughout.

**This case study's centrepiece is a control design rather than a severity number.** The reconstruction's *primary* synthetic sweep does not survive its own control, and the experiment that shows this — a 2x2x2 factorial that decomposes the sweep's estimate into the three construction choices that produced it — is the most reusable result in this paper. We therefore lead with it, before the sweep it corrects.

#### 4.3.1 A factorial that decomposes a false-positive severity estimate

The shipped control had been disadvantaged against LEAKY in three ways at once: a selection set smaller than the fold it stands in for, a selection run trained on less data than LEAKY's, and a systematically different stopping depth. Parts of that had been probed one factor at a time, but no design separated them, and none touched training depth — so nothing could say whether the gap was bought by *when* the honest arm stops or by *how* its stopping point was chosen. `code/77` crosses all three at capacity 128 and AUROC_0=0.80, n=100 seeds, with every arm sharing one pool, one fold assignment and one LEAKY reference, so all eight gaps are paired on identical folds. The factors are selection-set size (|rm sel|=56, the shipped value, against |rm sel|=|`val_idx`|~94), selection-run training budget (in-fold, so the selection run trains on 75–85% of `tr_idx`, against out-of-fold at 100%), and training depth (each arm's own selected epoch, against that epoch shifted by the single integer equalizing the arm's mean depth with LEAKY's). Two of the eight cells are arms `code/75` already shipped and are asserted per seed against it before anything is reported; so are LEAKY and PLACEBO.

**Selection-set size does three quarters of the work on paper, but only one of the three shares is actually distinguishable from zero.** Averaging each factor over the other two, giving the honest arm fewer selection points than LEAKY is worth +0.0023 (75.5% of the total movement from the shipped cell to the corrected one), training its selection run on less data is worth +0.0009 (28.1%), and letting it stop at a different mean depth is worth -0.0002 (-6.2%, i.e. it very slightly *suppresses* the gap). The design is essentially additive: the three main effects and the total movement disagree by +0.00008. **None of these three percentages carried an uncertainty interval until a seed-level bootstrap over the paired factorial arrays was run (`code/85`, 10,000 resamples of the 100 seeds, jackknife-estimated BCa acceleration).** Only the selection-set-size share clears zero: 75.5%, BCa 95% CI [+40.7%,+253.6%] (on the underlying effect, +0.0023, CI [+0.0012,+0.0035], both excluding zero). The other two do not: the selection-run-budget share is 28.1% but BCa [-141.3%,+61.4%] (effect +0.0009, CI [-0.0012,+0.0030]), and the training-depth share is -6.2% but BCa [-31.4%,+2.3%] (effect -0.0002, CI [-0.0004,+0.0000]) — both intervals cover zero, so neither share should be read as an established quantity, only as the point estimate of a ratio whose own denominator (the total movement, itself BCa [+0.0007,+0.0057], excluding zero) is well away from zero but whose numerator is not. **The only share this factorial actually establishes is that selection-set size accounts for a strict majority of the movement; it does not establish that the budget factor accounts for over a quarter, only that it is compatible with anywhere from a small negative to a large positive contribution.** The depth factor's near-nullity is unsurprising in hindsight — the arms' mean selected epochs are 20.37 (LEAKY) against 20.94–21.22, under one epoch in forty-five — but it is the only measurement in this paper bearing at all on the adaptivity question of §4.3.4, and it does not settle it: varying *when* an arm stops is not the same as varying whether its stopping rule was adaptive. What it does establish is that the depth difference between the shipped control and LEAKY explains none of the shipped gap.

**That decomposition is the result, not the null.** A null on its own would be uninformative — it could mean the effect is absent, or that the corrected design lost power. A null whose entire distance from the shipped estimate is attributed to two named, measurable construction choices is a reusable methodological finding: it says precisely which detail of a matched-control design manufactures a false positive of this size, and it does so on a design where every arm is paired on the same folds. Under the factorial's pre-registered rule — gap >0 with a BCa interval excluding zero — the fold-reuse-specific severity of Mechanism 3 in this harness is **not established**.

**Is this decomposition an artifact of `ES_HOLD_FRACTION`=0.15 specifically?** Appendix~ flags this constant as generating the largest number of disclosed confounds in this paper and notes that 0.25 (the exact fold match) "would match the fold exactly at the cost of a larger budget deficit; we did not run it," naming this an open robustness gap. `code/100` reruns `code/77`'s identical factorial at `ES_HOLD_FRACTION`in0.05,0.10,0.15,0.20, holding every other factor fixed; 0.25 and 0.30 both turn out to be infeasible under this factorial's shared out-of-fold pool (its ~94-point pool cannot supply the small/out-of-fold arm's required ~93–112 selection points without a stratified split of size 1, a sharper ceiling than expected before the sweep — see Appendix~ for the detail). **The fully corrected cell does not move**: -0.0001 at every value tested, exactly as it must, since it never depends on this constant. **The shipped gap and the total movement shrink together as `ES_HOLD_FRACTION` grows** (shipped +0.0104->+0.0057->+0.0030->+0.0024 for 0.05->0.10->0.15->0.20), which is mechanical: a larger `ES_HOLD_FRACTION` makes the "small" selection set less different from the fold-matched one, shrinking the asymmetry there is to explain. **The selection-set-size share is not stable across this range**: 74.7%, 69.0%, 75.5% (the shipped value) and 38.5% at 0.05, 0.10, 0.15 and 0.20 respectively, with the selection-run-budget share rising correspondingly (16.6%,31.4%,28.1%,62.5%) to fill the difference — consistent with the "small" and "fold_matched" levels converging as `ES_HOLD_FRACTION` approaches the exact fold match at 0.25, so this share is the *least* stable of the three quantities in the decomposition, not the most. **Read plainly, this sweep does not support treating 75.5% as invariant to this undisclosed choice**: it holds up over 0.05–0.15 but not beyond, so Appendix~'s framing of this constant as the paper's most disclosed-confound-generating one is, if anything, an understatement — it extends to the factorial's own decomposition, not only to the qualitative NOT-established verdict, which every value tested agrees on.

Two things bound how far that reaches. First, what disappears is the *residual on top of honest checkpoint selection*, not honest selection's value: the fully corrected arm still beats the placebo by +0.0124 to +0.0361 at every capacity (p<10^-4 throughout). Second, the out-of-fold design runs at a 16.7% smaller effective training pool than the primary estimate (matched across its own arms), and this paper's own Sweeps B and D disagree on the sign of that pool-size effect; the in-fold fold-matched arm does not carry it and points the same way, which is why we treat the two together as decisive enough to report and not decisive enough to retract the mechanism.

*Why "matched depth" is a shift rather than a constant.* Forcing every arm to one fixed epoch count is degenerate: a blind fixed-epoch retrain from a fixed initialization is a function of the epoch count alone, so all four constant-depth arms would be the same weights and the factorial would collapse to one cell. `code/77` asserts that identity at runtime and reports the constant-depth arm once, as the last row of Table~, where it measures something else worth knowing: **at this operating point LEAKY is 0.0026 AUROC *below* a control that does no checkpoint selection whatsoever** (p=0.003). What the placebo comparison calls honest selection's "value" is value relative to a noise-selecting baseline, not relative to not selecting at all.

The real-feature harness feeds real Mistral-7B/HaluEval features (n=400, MultiHaluDet's own unmodified feature-extraction code, a 4-bit AWQ checkpoint pinned to a fixed revision) through a 5-fold out-of-fold-plus-meta-learner reconstruction of the audited architecture, with the same three conditions. Read raw, every condition operates at AUROC ~0.985, where a task with no room to fail leaves no room for a leakage bug to show inflation. **Calibrating down to the synthetic sweep's operating point uses a fully label-free procedure**: class-mean axis, midpoint and pooled within-class SD estimated from train indices only, then one identical noising map applied to every row. Two label-dependent alternatives are disqualified on inspection — a rescaling transform centring class means on the *full* labeled sample before any split existed (cos=-0.9999 at alpha=0, train-fit test-scored AUROC 0.06), and a residual label-conditional step reading each test point's own label to choose its class offset (0.584+/-0.059 where chance is correct) — both instantiating exactly the pattern §6's checklist names (Appendix~, issues 8 and 13). The label-free version reaches chance exactly at zero separation (0.487+/-0.092), and the alpha search converges to alpha=0.3359 at CV AUROC 0.7995 against the 0.80 target, reaching its intended operating point rather than saturating above it. Under the downstream pipeline the operating point still lands at ~0.94–0.95 (LEAKY 0.9403 at capacity 128, 0.9460 at 384), so a direct magnitude comparison against the synthetic sweep's 0.80 harness remains invalid and we make none. At its own operating point the shipped comparison is strongly significant (Wilcoxon p=7.3x10^-7 and 1.4x10^-5, paired permutation p<0.0001), and CLEAN_MATCHED beats PLACEBO decisively at both capacities (+0.0151, p=9.4x10^-7; +0.0091, p=1.0x10^-5); the paired sign-flip permutation test accompanies Wilcoxon throughout because these differences contain many ties (33 and 40 of 100) on a discrete 80-sample split. A separate FP16-vs-AWQ control is consistent with no quantization confound rather than ruling one out at this resolution (+0.0068, [-0.061,+0.080], p=0.148).

**The fidelity extension ports more of the audited trainer**, stepping a `ReduceLROnPlateau` scheduler on the reused fold's AUROC every epoch and tying early stopping to the same signal (`code/49`), at a closely matching operating point (LEAKY mean 0.9424 against 0.9403). **Coupling continuity is not what raises that number, and we no longer claim it is:** an isolating arm with only a final-checkpoint argmax **outscores** continuous coupling by -0.0159 (BCa [-0.0209,-0.0124], Wilcoxon p=2.8x10^-13; 0.9424 coupled against 0.9583 argmax-only), because early stopping at `patience`=15 truncates the leaky arm to a mean epoch of 29.14 against 40.73. As implemented, continuous coupling subtracts by undertraining the arm, and the like-for-like ratio against the checkpoint-only harness (2.7x rather than 3.6x) is a statement about two harnesses, not about coupling. Two scope limits remain: the arms still differ in training depth as well as validation signal (29.14 against 19.75, +47.6%, p=7.2x10^-13; placebo 7.95), reported rather than equalized because choosing when to stop *is* the leaked decision this extension models; and "fidelity" here means fidelity of the validation-signal coupling and optimizer schedule, not of the loss or model (weight EMA, composite objective, rebalancing, augmentation and model class remain unported), so this estimates a fold-reuse severity *within this paper's own harness*, not the inflation of MultiHaluDet's reported number.

**Reading Table~: the two corrections do not have the same standing, and the result is capacity-dependent.** Under the *fold-matched* carve-out, directly comparable to the shipped figure, every interval stays clear of zero — the real-feature estimate retains 65% and 87% of its magnitude and the fidelity extension attenuates 36% at capacity 128, the one place the correction leaves the qualitative conclusion intact — and **we report the fold-matched figures as those harnesses' severities.** Under the *fully corrected* out-of-fold control (intervals 1.2–2.6x wider, for removing 1/(K+1) of the pool to buy its symmetry), the effect is *marginally* established at capacity 384 in both harnesses (real-feature BCa lower bound +0.00003, three parts in 10^5) and at 128 in neither, directionally consistent but underpowered there. The interval and Wilcoxon criteria disagree at both capacity-384 cells (Wilcoxon p=0.187, 57/100 seeds positive, real-feature; a near-coin flip p=0.9155, 44/100, fidelity extension), and Holm correction across all four cells leaves none significant (p_Holm>=0.68); per §3's rule we rest the verdict on BCa alone, reporting it as directionally consistent rather than doubly established. The fidelity extension's magnitude is itself strongly capacity-dependent, falling 4.8x from capacity 128 to 384 under the shipped control (3.6x fold-matched) — a further reason not to quote either figure as a mechanism-level severity (§5.2). Which constituent of the asymmetry dominates is also harness-specific: on real features the selection-run budget dominates (+0.0039, +0.0034) and depth matters (+0.0033, +0.0022), while selection-set size contributes little or negatively (+0.0004, -0.0022) — the reverse of the synthetic harness, so a reader should not carry that decomposition over to their own pipeline.

**Is the capacity-dependent decline monotone, or a two-point-grid artifact?** `code/96` reruns the LEAKY/CLEAN_MATCHED harness on the identical cached real features at a denser grid, 64,128,256,384, under a simplified single-fold readout (§5.3) and both the shipped-style and fully-corrected controls. The shipped-style gaps are +0.0028, +0.0028, +0.0002, +0.0007 — **not monotone**: capacity 256 is the smallest, with 384 partially rebounding. The fully-corrected gaps are -0.0004, +0.0003, -0.0003, -0.0002 — indistinguishable from zero throughout, consistent with the primary harness's own finding of a marginal effect only at capacity 384, but here absent across the whole grid rather than emerging partially at one end. The fidelity extension's two-point decline (128, 384) therefore does not extend to a clean, monotone relationship across a denser grid: the intermediate capacities reveal non-monotonicity the original comparison could not see, so a reader should not extrapolate a smooth capacity law from the 4.8x/3.6x figures above.

**One supporting line is withdrawn rather than resized.** The synthetic harness, like MultiHaluDet's own trainer, averages five fold-models' test-set activations before the meta-learner scores them, so a fold-specific leaked signal could be largely cancelled before the reported metric sees it. Against the shipped control that suppression reproduces (2.3–5.8x across capacities; 3.0x at capacity 128 on an independent recomputation), so *averaging attenuates whatever gap is present* stands. But the claim it supported — that the mechanism is therefore not benign at its source — requires the un-averaged gap against a *sound* control to be non-zero, and at the per-fold-model readout it is not (+0.0015, interval covering zero, against +0.0089 versus the shipped control): what averaging suppressed there was largely the control asymmetry, not the leak. The one readout where a gap survives correction is the OOF matrix read directly (+0.0059, [+0.0044,+0.0075]), in-sample for every arm and so usable comparatively but not a performance estimate. We report the source-versus-reported distinction as real in direction and much smaller than first suggested, and withdraw this line from the evidence carrying the mechanism's severity.

**A second model family, same dataset: does severity transport?** We extracted the identical feature set from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ (same unmodified extraction code, same 400 HaluEval samples; model revision not pinned for this exploratory run) via a Kaggle T4 kernel, run through this paper's own four-arm pipeline at capacity 128, 100 seeds. Qwen2.5-7B reaches AUROC ~0.983–0.986 across all four conditions (leaky 0.9834, clean 0.9835, clean_matched 0.9836, placebo 0.9828), even closer to ceiling than Mistral's ~0.94–0.95. The checkpoint-selection gap is -0.0002 (BCa interval covering zero, Wilcoxon p=0.42) — **indistinguishable from zero, one sign away from even being positive.** This is not evidence against the mechanism transporting to a second model family; it is exactly what §5.3's operating-point relationship predicts this close to ceiling, extending that relationship's evidence to a second architecture at a near-ceiling point rather than testing whether severity grows with model capability at a *matched* operating point (a comparison not run here). We report it as one additional data point consistent with the operating-point account, not as resolving whether severity increases with harness fidelity or model expressiveness more generally (Limitation 8's "fidelity gradient" remains open on that).

#### 4.3.4 Alternative explanations, the control family, and the bottom line

**Does the estimate depend on the generative process?** A second process built on the real anisotropic within-class covariance of the Mistral-7B/HaluEval features, rescaled to the same AUROC=0.80 target (`code/27`), gives +0.0082 (p=0.007) at capacity 128 and +0.0099 (p=0.009) at 384 while weakening at the two lower capacities; pooling all eight synthetic tests into one Holm-Bonferroni family leaves two significant, both capacity-128 cells. A third holds d=64, the eigenbasis, the total within-class variance, the mean-difference direction and the calibrated Mahalanobis J fixed and varies *only* the eigenvalue profile (`code/56`): severity at capacity 128 rises monotonically with anisotropy, +0.0015 (gamma=0, an exact isotropic control, not significant) through +0.0038 and +0.0044 to +0.0104 (gamma=2), `real_top64` at +0.0048. The isotropic assumption alone is not responsible for the estimate. **Two limits:** the capacity-by-capacity significance pattern reshuffles rather than replicating, and the pooled verdict moved in our own favour when our own instrument was corrected, which deserves the most scrutiny, not the least; and anisotropy is perfectly rank-confounded with the achieved operating point in the third experiment (Spearman -1.00 at capacity 128, no monotone pattern at 384), so it cannot distinguish "anisotropy raises severity" from "anisotropy lowers the operating point, which raises severity."

**Is it adaptive selection at all, rather than fold reuse? Unanswered.** CLEAN_MATCHED_ADAPTIVE (`code/22`) ran LEAKY's *ongoing* adaptive selection from a disjoint carve-out, and its increments over the blind control looked like evidence the effect is fold-reuse-specific. It is not: that arm and the budget-deficit arm CLEAN_MATCHED_85 (`code/67`) are the same weights — same data, same torch seed, same stopping epoch, and full-batch deterministic training makes those one trajectory, verified *bitwise* in 15/15 (seed, fold) pairs at capacity 128 with AUROC means agreeing to the last float digit (0.7555346938775511). The increment read as adaptivity-specific is arithmetically the training-budget deficit: +0.0015 at capacity 128 ([-0.0003,+0.0035], p=0.29) and +0.0031 at 384 ([+0.0004,+0.0057], p=0.005) — 31% and 54% of those increments, at 384 exceeding the whole LEAKY-minus-CLEAN_MATCHED gap of +0.0027. We withdraw the adaptivity conclusion; the only measurement bearing on the question is the factorial's depth factor, worth -0.0002, and depth is not adaptivity.

**The severity is control-relative, so we report the spread across the control family rather than one member of it.** At capacity 128 the same leaky procedure on the same metric gives +0.0034 against a blind retrain on the full fold (CLEAN_MATCHED) and +0.0049 against the same blind retrain on the 85% subset its epoch count was chosen on — the two members of the admissible family, since the adaptive arm reproduces the second bitwise — spanning 1.45x; a contaminated adaptive control we disqualify on independent grounds gives +0.0198, making it 5.9x if counted. **The disqualification is the point rather than a rescue:** it is a judgement about which counterfactual is admissible, and that judgement, not the mechanism or the metric, is what fixes the number. (Both figures come from a superseded coupled-seed scheme the factorial does not use; `code/22`'s per-seed arrays are bit-identical to the shipped legacy JSON and differ from the decoupled arrays by up to 0.16 per seed, which is why the capacity-128 gap reads +0.0036 in Table~ and +0.0034 here.) The rule pre-registered before the sweep — `GENUINE_LEAK_CONFIRMED`/`CONFOUND_CONFIRMED_NO_REAL_LEAK`/`MIXED` — is reachable in every direction but not *stable*: the same mechanism yields all three depending on generative process, calibration and capacity, and the verdict moved in the *favourable* direction when the calibration was corrected, which is when a single pre-registered label deserves least weight.

**Bottom line.** The code pattern is unambiguous and verified directly against the pinned commit; its severity, measured against a corrected control, is not established in the primary synthetic harness (Table , a located null against the shipped control, not a detectable effect once corrected) and is small and capacity-dependent on real features (Table~'s fold-matched severities). None of this settles MultiHaluDet's own 98.55% AUROC's inflation: their architecture (6 transformer layers, multi-scale, heavy augmentation) is considerably more expressive than this reconstruction.

#### 4.3.5 An oracle baseline for the fold-reuse control: is the fully-corrected estimator itself biased?

Table~'s correction sequence (shipped -> fold-matched -> fully corrected) is monotone non-increasing across all four rows with no sign of convergence, and Limitation (8) already names the resulting worry: a fourth correction round could in principle drive the surviving real-feature severities to zero too, the way the 2x2x2 factorial did the primary synthetic sweep. That worry is about the *estimator*, not only about the data, and it is exactly the question Case Study 4's calibration (\S) answers for the winner's-curse estimators by building a setting where the true selection bias is known by Monte Carlo construction. `code/87` does the analogous construction for Mechanism 3: a two-way decomposition over training epochs e and independent runs r, v(r,e)=mu(e)+s_r+varepsilon_val(r,e) for the reused validation fold's score and c(r,e)=mu(e)+s_r+varepsilon_sel(r,e) for a disjoint carve-out's, with mu(e) a rising-then-plateauing learning curve (epochs are not exchangeable, matching \S5.3's own point) and s_r a per-run main effect constant in e. LEAKY selects hat e=argmax_e v(r,e) and is scored on the same reused fold, so its true bias is the winner's-curse term mathbbE[varepsilon_val(r,hat e)], known only by Monte Carlo, exactly as \S's bias_true. The fold-matched and fully-corrected arms instead select hat e=argmax_e c(r,e) — a noise draw independent of varepsilon_val — and are then scored on the reused fold, so their true bias is mathbbE[varepsilon_val(r,hat e)] evaluated at an index chosen independently of varepsilon_val: **exactly zero, by independence alone, with no free parameter to tune it there.** This is the toy-model analogue of \S's known-zero null, but here it is the default condition for any control that selects off-fold, not a limiting case reached only at extreme separation.

Calibrating sigma_val to this paper's own measured dispersion (`code/47`'s Sweep~A, K=45, capacity 128: mean_val_auc_std=0.0343) and reading sigma at the sample sizes \S4.3 actually uses (n_val=94, n_sel=56 for the shipped arm, 94 for fold-matched/fully-corrected) via the standard 1/sqrt(n) AUROC-variance scaling, 400,000-draw Monte Carlo gives a true bias of +0.0664 for the shipped in-fold construction (its selection statistic is a noisy *subsample* of the very fold it is later scored on, so the two noise draws are positively correlated rather than independent) against -0.00005 (fold-matched) and +0.00003 (fully corrected) — both zero to Monte-Carlo precision, as the independence argument requires. Simulating 600 repeated 100-seed studies at each condition, the fully-corrected estimator's own BCa 95% interval covers this true value in 94.2% of simulated studies (nominal 95%), with a point-estimate bias of only +0.00009 against a true value of 0.00003; fold-matched covers it in 97.5% of studies. Repeating the check across a five-point contrast sweep (the learning curve's plateau height scaled 0.25x to 4x, from a barely-separated to a strongly-separated regime) leaves both the fold-matched and fully-corrected true biases at zero throughout, while the shipped construction's true bias stays large and positive at every point (+0.057 to +0.072).

**The answer this settles is narrower than "the real-feature numbers are real," and we state the scope precisely.** What it establishes: the fully-corrected out-of-fold *estimator*, at the sample sizes this paper's own harness uses, is calibrated for the fold-reuse-selection bias it targets, with interval coverage close to nominal in a setting built so the true answer is known — so the surviving real-feature severities (Table~) are *not* an artifact of the correction procedure itself drifting toward zero, the concern Limitation (8) raises. What it does not establish: this toy model has no analogue of the selection-set-size/budget/depth confounds the 2x2x2 factorial found responsible for most of the *synthetic* sweep's shipped gap (Table~), nor of any anisotropic or non-Gaussian structure specific to real Mistral-7B features. A fourth correction round on real features could still find a real confound of exactly that kind; this section only rules out one specific candidate explanation, that the estimator itself is biased, and rules it out because independence of the two noise sources makes the true bias zero by construction rather than by any tuned cancellation.

### 4.4 Case Study 4: test-set-driven best-layer selection (quantized-LLM paper) — +0.0021 AUROC as computed, +0.0029 to +0.0040 after calibration along an identified joint manifold (all 24 published cells)

[See `draft/case_study_quantized_llm_paper.md` for full detail, including literal code excerpts.]

"Hallucination Is Linearly Decodable from Mid-Layer Hidden States in Quantized LLMs" (Aiersilan 2026; arXiv 2606.02628; repo `github.com/Ezharjan/HallucinationPatternDetection`, pinned at commit `ea0b9678`) reports AUROC up to 1.000. Its per-layer probe training uses a clean single 70/10/20 split, genuinely free of Case Studies 1–3's patterns. But `src/detection/saplma.py::saplma_probe_per_layer` selects a `best_layer` by the argmax, over every probed layer, of each layer's **test-set** AUROC, then reports that same test-set number as the headline with no correction for having tried that many hypotheses against the one test set — the classical winner's curse. The number of probed layers is model-dependent and not constant across the 24 cells: 33 for the eight `llama3.1-8b` and eight `mistral-7b` cells, 29 for the eight `qwen2.5-7b` cells, with `code/45` asserting these counts against the shipped files so the figure cannot drift. Re-quantifying severity does not require re-running their quantized checkpoint: their repo ships per-layer, per-seed AUROC arrays for all 24 model/dataset/probe-type combinations, 3 seeds each. For each combination, each seed is held out in turn, the best layer is selected using *only* the other two, the naive baseline is the selection-set mean at that layer, and the honest estimate is the held-out seed's AUROC at the same layer.

**Two theorems govern what this section may claim, and we state them before any number: both candidate estimators are non-negative for any data whatsoever.** Neither one's sign is therefore evidence that an effect exists, and every figure below is a *magnitude*. *Theorem~ (rotation estimator).* Delta_wcge0 for every real Lx R matrix, Rge2: writing f(r,l) for seed r's deviation from the grand mean at layer l, the cell estimate reduces to tfrac1R-1sum_r f(r,L_r), and each rotation's selected layer L_r being optimal for that rotation's own criterion forces the sum non-negative. The equal-argmax degeneracy pinning 7 of the 24 cells to exactly zero is the *equality case* of this theorem, not a separate defect. *Theorem~ (bootstrap max-bias estimator).* Delta_bootge0 for every real matrix. Its two sides are computed on different seed multisets, so nothing telescopes — but a bootstrap resample of seeds is mean-preserving at each fixed layer and the maximum is convex, so Jensen's inequality pins mathbbE^*[max_l c^*(l)]gemax_lbar m(l) while trivially mathbbE^*[bar m(L^*)]lemax_lbar m(l); subtracting gives the result, with equality exactly when the grand-mean argmax is bootstrap-stable. Proofs and their numerical verification — 300,000 random matrices over five distributional families for the first (minimum observed -2.3x10^-10, float noise) and 50,000 by exact enumeration for the second (minimum -1.6x10^-14) — are in Appendix~.

**Three consequences, which together are this section's methodological stance.** *First, uniform positivity is algebra and not evidence.* "All 17 non-degenerate cells are positive" restates the theorem, and the Wilcoxon p=1.5x10^-5 quoted at n=17 is exactly 2^-16, the arithmetic floor of the signed-rank test when every observation shares a sign. We withdraw every inference this paper drew from either estimator's sign, including the pooled Wilcoxon p=6.0x10^-7 attached to Delta_boot. *Second, the estimator is enumerated rather than sampled.* `code/45` estimated Delta_boot from 2,000 random resamples when, at three seeds, there are exactly 3^3=27 equiprobable ones and the estimator has a closed form; its per-cell Monte-Carlo error reached 6.8x10^-4, enough to return two cells as negative where exact enumeration puts them at -1.1x10^-16 and +3.3x10^-17 — and those two are precisely the bootstrap-stable cells the theorem pins to zero. *Third, since sign carries nothing, a number here is licensed by calibration against a known ground truth and an inference by a permutation null.* On that basis we report Delta_boot as primary — it resamples three seeds, matching the criterion the audited repository actually selects on — and retain Delta_wc as a non-negative diagnostic, useful for ranking cells and locating the degeneracy but carrying no evidence that an effect exists.

**The numbers, as computed**, by exact enumeration:

| Cell subset | Delta_boot | Delta_wc (retired) |
|---|---|---|
| All 24 published cells | +0.0021 (max. +0.0122; 0 negative) | +0.0054 |
| 17 rotation-visible cells | +0.0028 | +0.0076 |
| 7 rotation-degenerate cells | +0.0005 | 0 (exact, by construction) |

2 of the 24 cells are exactly zero under Delta_boot, its own degeneracy set; on the 7 rotation-degenerate cells, five carry a real but smaller effect rather than the exact zero the rotation estimator assigns them, while the other two are degenerate under both. **All four figures are non-negative by construction and are reported as magnitudes.** The BCa intervals across the 24 cells ([+0.0014,+0.0037] for Delta_boot, [+0.0031,+0.0101] for Delta_wc) describe the *spread across published cells* and, per the calibration below, do not cover the true bias; a dataset-clustered interval, which respects the fact that these 24 cells are one crossed design rather than 24 independent observations, is 20% wider at [+0.0009,+0.0038]. All 24 per-cell values of both estimators are in Appendix~, table relocated to `EXTENDED_TECHNICAL_DETAIL.md`. **Two further sensitivities move the diagnostic and we disclose both.** (i)~*Tie breaking.* `np.argmax` resolves ties at the lowest index; 11 of the 24 cells have at least one rotation with tied maxima, so the shipped "7 of 24 degenerate" is really 6 to 11 of 24, and enumerating every tie resolution moves the all-24 diagnostic mean over [+0.0046,+0.0057] — a band whose *top* is where the shipped convention lands. (ii)~*A selection-criterion mismatch.* The audited repository picks its `best_layer` on a mean over all three seeds; the rotation estimator picks on a two-seed mean, a strictly noisier criterion, which incurs a larger winner's curse than the repository actually carries. Calibrating that gap cell by cell (`code/59`) puts the overstatement at 21% on average (range 18 to 32%). The bootstrap estimator resamples three seeds and is unaffected by *this* mismatch; it is affected by a different one, which the calibration below identifies.

**Calibration against a known ground truth, which is what licenses reporting a number at all.** The theorems say these estimators cannot be negative; neither says whether either recovers the right *size*. `code/71` generates data from a two-way model at the audited design (L=33, R=3) in which the true selection bias is known by construction and sweeps it down to *exactly zero* with seed noise still present; at that zero condition all three estimators return 0 to float precision and none is ever negative, as the theorems require. Two marginal sweeps — one over interaction correlation rho, one over layer separation — disagreed about the calibration factor, because the high-rho, high-separation corner where the real data sits is simulated by neither. `code/81`'s joint (rho,sep) sweep identifies it and resolves the disagreement: reading the ratio along the *manifold* of cells reproducing the observed Delta_boot=+0.0021 to within 10%, under the 24 real cells' own plug-in per-layer mu profiles that manifold is **well identified** — two cells qualify, their ratios span only 0.545–0.574x (a factor of 1.05), and the implied true bias is +0.0037 to +0.0038 — while under a flat mu it is **degenerate**, three cells qualifying with ratios spanning 2.7–10.1x. That difference is the point rather than a contradiction: matching on the observed estimator value alone does not identify the condition unless the layer-mean profile is held at the real one, and the marginal separation sweep varied separation under a flat profile the real data does not have. Along the real-profile manifold the three estimators' corrected values are +0.0037–+0.0039, +0.0029–+0.0030 and +0.0039–+0.0040, so we report +0.0021 as the directly computed magnitude and +0.0029 to +0.0040 as the calibration-corrected range. We do *not* report the BCa interval as a confidence interval for the severity: in simulation it covers the true bias in 0% of runs for both bootstrap estimators and 37–77% for Delta_wc (Appendix~); the chronology of the two marginal sweeps' disagreement is in `EXTENDED_TECHNICAL_DETAIL.md`.

**Where this case study's inferential weight sits: a permutation null, read in both tails and for both estimators.** A statistic two theorems forbid from going negative can be tested against nothing but a reference that retains genuine variability, and the per-layer seed-shuffle null is the only such reference the shipped three-seed arrays admit. `code/66` runs it with the bootstrap max-bias as the statistic and — because three seeds admit exactly 27 equiprobable resamples — evaluates that statistic by enumerating all 27 rather than sampling 2,000, removing the estimator's own Monte-Carlo error from both sides; the exact all-24 mean is +0.002107 against the superseded +0.002069, so the headline +0.0021 is unchanged by the switch. **The result, stated once and by tail.** In the *upper* tail, **0 of the 24 cells** reach p<0.05 — true of both estimators, the smallest upper-tail p being Delta_wc's 0.277 against a median of 1.000. Read *two-sided*, Delta_boot has 16 of 24 cells significant (minimum p=0.0010, the smallest attainable at 2,000 permutations) and Delta_wc has 11 of 24 — every one of them in the *below* direction, none above. Counting the below direction one-sided gives 20 of 24 for Delta_boot (13 of the 17 non-degenerate) and 12 of 24 for Delta_wc (10 of 17). The non-degenerate cells' own null mean of +0.0118 likewise sits *above* their observed +0.0076.

**That reference cuts in both directions, and how to read it follows from how it is built.** Shuffling within layers destroys exactly the structure that makes an argmax stable, putting the estimator in its maximal-winner's-curse regime, so a real but modest effect on a stable argmax is *expected* to sit below it. That is what makes the null diagnostic for the 7 rotation-degenerate cells: they sit at exactly 0.0000 under Delta_wc while their own null averages +0.0179, *above* the non-degenerate cells' observed mean — evidence those exact zeros reflect a stable argmax defeating the estimator rather than an absent effect, corroborated by Delta_boot's +0.0005 on five of the same seven cells. By the same construction the 16-of-24 two-sided count is *not* evidence of significance in any useful sense: sitting significantly below a maximally-inflated null is what a conservative estimator on a stable argmax is predicted to do. What the null does establish is the weaker and correct statement: *no cell is significantly above its own null*, so this reference establishes neither +0.0076 nor +0.0021 as an upward effect, and the theorems say neither could have been established by its sign either. With three seeds per cell no sharper null is available, and none of this disturbs the theorems, which are exact and depend on no null.

**Excluding the rotation-degenerate cells is itself a selection, and it cuts against us.** A cell is rotation-degenerate exactly when its argmax is stable across all three rotations, which is evidence the layer ranking is driven by real structure rather than noise — the regime in which a winner's curse is *genuinely smaller* — so the 17 surviving cells are plausibly enriched for a larger true effect and +0.0076 is the diagnostic's value where it has resolving power, not an unbiased average over all 24. Delta_boot needs no *equal-argmax* exclusion, putting five of those seven at +0.0004 to +0.0014, but has its own degeneracy set of two, so "needs no exclusion" holds on 22 of 24; its 24 values have SD 0.0027 and a pooled minimum detectable effect of +0.0015 at 80% power (Delta_wc: 0.0079 and +0.0045), and the non-degenerate non-saturated subgroup (n=5) is plainly underpowered, so we report it descriptively and do not lean on its p=0.0625.

**Ceiling composition of the 24 cells.** These cells are not comparable to each other: in 12 of 24 the *selected* layer already sits at AUROC >=0.975, leaving almost no headroom. Under Delta_boot the split is ceiling-saturated +0.0016 ([+0.0013,+0.0019]) against non-saturated +0.0026 ([+0.0011,+0.0056]), a 1.6x difference with no sub-selection on either side; the retired estimator gives 1.5x and Delta_boot^std gives 5.5x. **The direction is robust across all three estimators and the multiplier is not**, so we quote 1.5–5.5x and name the estimator behind each end. Severity is small on every slicing and larger where there is room for it to show, consistent with §5's operating-point relationship. **What Case Study 4 supports** is a severity *magnitude* of +0.0021 as computed and +0.0029 to +0.0040 after calibration, together with the qualitative claim that the audited selection rule is biased upward — which follows from the mechanism, not from the estimator's sign. It supports no calibrated interval, and nothing at all that rests on either estimator being positive.

### 4.5 Mechanism 5: test-set-driven operating-point selection (MultiHaluDet) — found while auditing Case Study 3's own repository

A fifth, structurally distinct mechanism sits in the exact file this paper already audits for Mechanism 3. `MultiHaluDet/run_pipeline.py::stage_4_ensemble` calls `find_best_thresholds(probs, y_test)`, which sweeps 81 candidate F1 thresholds and the ROC-optimal (Youden) threshold *directly against the test labels*, then reports every threshold-dependent metric — F1, accuracy, Matthews correlation, Cohen's kappa, balanced accuracy, and the ECE computed from those predictions — at that test-label-selected threshold. AUROC is threshold-free and unaffected; every other headline number that pipeline reports is test-set-optimized operating-point selection. Mechanisms 1–4 all concern *which feature, checkpoint, or layer* to use; this one concerns *which threshold to report at*, given a fixed model. **It is also not specific to this repository**: a blind re-read (§6) found the same pattern in Case Study 4's, at `HallucinationPatternDetection/src/analysis/metrics.py:57-65`, which picks a Youden threshold by `np.argmax(tpr-fpr)` on the same labels it then scores `accuracy_at_best_thr` and `f1_at_best_thr` against, with `scripts/07_aggregate_results.py:85-86` reporting both into the headline table, verified against the pinned commit. Two independently published pipelines committing the identical pattern is what makes Mechanism 5 worth naming separately.

We quantify it with the same isotropic-Gaussian calibration this paper's other estimates rely on, with a logistic-regression classifier in place of the full architecture since threshold-selection leakage does not depend on which classifier produced the probabilities (`code/46`); that generator is verified against a dedicated, non-tautological calibration guardrail (`code/sanity_checks.py`), which catches the calibration-inversion error class documented in Appendix~ for this paper's own tooling. **Which threshold the gap is measured at is not a free choice**: `run_pipeline.py:139-140` passes only `thresholds['youden']` to `evaluate_all`, so *every* threshold-dependent metric MultiHaluDet reports, F1 included, is scored at Youden, while the F1-argmax threshold is computed and discarded — and `code/46` measured the F1 gap at the discarded one. `code/62` re-measures at the repo-faithful rule, 200 seeds per cell, everything else unchanged, re-emitting the F1-argmax column as a check that it reproduces `code/46`'s shipped cells exactly (it does, to float64). **The correction moves the finding in this paper's favour on both counts that matter.** *First, the effect is larger:* F1 gaps run from +0.0225 (0.985, MultiHaluDet's own reported operating point) to +0.0520 (target 0.70), with no clean monotonic trend (0.70->0.0520, 0.80->0.0431, 0.90->0.0348, 0.95->0.0262, 0.985->0.0225), every cell significant at p<5x10^-8 with BCa 95% CIs excluding zero throughout; the F1-argmax figures (+0.0212 to +0.0338) *understate* Mechanism 5 as the pipeline actually reports it, by up to 2.28x at the low-AUROC end, and accuracy gaps (already scored at Youden by `code/46`) remain +0.0215 to +0.0457. *Second, the measurement becomes a measurement:* at the F1-argmax rule the gap is non-negative by algebra, LEAKY's threshold being the argmax over the very 81-point grid the test F1 is scored on and HONEST's drawn from that same grid, so LEAKY cannot lose (0/1000 negative reps) and its p-values encoded only n and the tie structure — whereas Youden maximizes tpr-fpr over `roc_curve`'s own threshold set, a different objective on a different candidate set, and LEAKY genuinely loses on test F1 in 67/37/20/11/4 of 200 reps at 0.70 through 0.985.

**These gaps are a function of sample size, and the sample sizes were fixed and unstated.** All five cells run at N=700, hence n_test=140, and a threshold chosen by argmax over an 81-point grid is a winner's curse over a criterion whose noise scales with 1/sqrt(n) — so "MultiHaluDet's own reported regime" matches their operating point and *not* their sample size, which their released configuration does not pin. Holding the operating point at 0.985 and scaling N (`code/62`) gives F1 gaps of +0.0225, +0.0123, +0.0066 and +0.0036 at n_test=140, 350, 700 and 2000 — a **6.3x decline**, close to the 3.8x a pure 1/sqrt(n) account gives — and +0.0212, +0.0111, +0.0067, +0.0034 (6.2x) under the F1-argmax convention, so the sample-size story is the same under either rule. Every cell remains overwhelmingly significant, so the mechanism does not vanish, but \textbf{the +0.022 to +0.052 range is an estimate at n_test=140, and a pipeline reporting at five times that size should expect roughly a third of it, at fourteen times roughly a sixth.}

**The two arms' selection sets are not the same size, and that was not disclosed.** LEAKY selects on the same n_test=140 points it is scored on; HONEST selects on n_val=112 and is scored on the same 140 — a 1.25x difference whose smaller, noisier set yields a worse threshold and so inflates the measured gap on top of the leakage it isolates. (§4.3 discloses the analogous 1.67x asymmetry for Mechanism 3; this one was not disclosed at all.) `code/74` sizes it with a third arm at n_val=n_test=140, 200 seeds, paired on seed: at 0.985 the size-matched F1 gap is +0.0214 (BCa [+0.0189,+0.0244], p=1.7x10^-32) against the shipped +0.0225, accuracy +0.0204 against +0.0215; the paired difference is -0.0012 for F1 ([-0.0041,+0.0016], p=0.67) and -0.0011 for accuracy (p=0.58), and isolating size alone from the training-budget cost leaves -0.0004 ([-0.0022,+0.0014], p=0.51). **So the asymmetry accounts for about 2% of the reported gap, with an interval straddling zero, and at most 8% at any of the five operating points.** It is real, it should have been disclosed, and it does not carry the finding.

**A necessary caveat on comparing this to Mechanism 3: these are not the same metric.** AUROC is threshold-free by construction, so Mechanism 5's severity on the one metric it shares with Mechanism 3 is not small but precisely nil. The F1 gap is *not* structurally guaranteed non-negative under the rule the audited pipeline uses — 139 of 1000 per-seed F1 gaps are negative — but for accuracy a version of that guarantee survives: Youden's J=2 bal.acc-1, so LEAKY's threshold exactly maximizes *balanced* accuracy over the very test set it is scored on, and on an exactly class-balanced split raw accuracy *is* balanced accuracy. The replicates bear this out — 5 of 1000 accuracy gaps are negative, all five on class-imbalanced splits, with 0 of the 105 exactly balanced replicates negative and minimum exactly 0.0000 — so we report accuracy as near-structurally non-negative rather than as independent confirmation of the F1 result. Mechanism 5 leaves the pipeline's reported AUROC entirely unaffected but materially inflates every threshold-dependent metric it also reports at that same selected threshold.

### 4.6 A composition test: when Mechanisms 3 and 5 co-occur, do their severities add?

Mechanisms 3 and 5 co-occur inside a single file of a single audited repository — MultiHaluDet's own `run_pipeline.py` both selects a checkpoint on a reused fold and a decision threshold on the test labels — which is how this paper found Mechanism 5 at all (\S5.1). Every severity number elsewhere in this paper measures one mechanism in isolation. This matters for a practitioner auditing a real pipeline, which typically carries more than one mechanism at once, so `code/93` builds a synthetic setting where both are present simultaneously and asks directly whether their severities on a shared metric (F1, the metric Mechanism 5 acts on) add, sub-additively combine, or interact.

The design is a 2x2 factorial, paired on identical data, seeds and folds throughout: checkpoint selection is either LEAKY (argmax on the reused validation fold) or CLEAN_MATCHED (argmax on a disjoint carve-out, retrained to the matched epoch count), crossed with a decision threshold that is either TEST-SELECTED (argmax F1 over an 81-point grid using the test labels themselves, \S4.5's own construction) or FIXED at 0.5. Writing M_3=F1(LEAKY,fixed)-F1(CLEAN_MATCHED,fixed) for Mechanism 3 alone and M_5=F1(CLEAN_MATCHED,test)-F1(CLEAN_MATCHED,fixed) for Mechanism 5 alone, at n=100 seeds, capacity 128, AUROC_0=0.80 (this paper's own flagship synthetic operating point): M_3=+0.0039 (BCa [-0.0019,+0.0097]) and M_5=+0.0526 ([+0.0472,+0.0584]) — Mechanism 5 dominates by more than an order of magnitude on this metric, consistent with \S4.5's own framing that F1/accuracy are exactly where Mechanism 5 shows its effect and AUROC is exactly where it cannot. With both mechanisms present at once, the combined gap is +0.0557 ([+0.0493,+0.0620]), against an additive prediction of M_3+M_5=+0.0565 ([+0.0475,+0.0663]); the interaction term (combined minus additive) is -0.0008 ([-0.0059,+0.0046], p=0.64), whose interval covers zero. **The two mechanisms combine additively in this reconstruction**, at this operating point and this metric: a practitioner who measures each mechanism's severity separately and sums them gets a number statistically indistinguishable from measuring both at once. We report this as one data point, not a law — a single operating point, one capacity, one generative process — and note that additivity is itself informative precisely because super- or sub-additivity were both live possibilities a priori (a shared downstream classifier could plausibly launder or compound either leak's effect on the other); this check rules neither alternative in without evidence, and finds none for either here.

## 5. What Governs Severity Within a Controlled Harness: Candidate Count and Operating Point

The five mechanisms above are structurally distinct, and identifying them is this paper's most durable contribution. Once each is measured against matched controls, Mechanisms 2–4's severities differ little from one another *on the absolute scale* — every code-verified estimate for those three lands in hundredths (Mechanism 1's own controlled reconstruction, §4.1, lands in tenths and is not part of this clustering) — and two continuous quantities, candidate count and operating point, move severity substantially *within* a fixed harness. This section states that finding and then bounds it hard: §5.2 places all three axes on one scale, and on the only comparison our own standards permit — ratios whose denominator's interval excludes zero — the mechanism-and-harness axis overlaps the operating-point axis substantially, and neither is ranked above the other on this evidence; §5.4 reports the transport check behind that conclusion. What survives is a within-harness claim, and the section is organized around establishing its scope rather than its reach.

### 5.1 The five mechanisms are points in a shared space, and their measured severities cluster

The three-axis map at the head of §4 locates the five mechanisms, and **the overlaps are the point: these are documented instances, not an orthogonal partition, and we do not claim otherwise.** Mechanisms 2 and 4 share axes (b) and (c) and differ *only* on (a); 4 and 5 share (a) and (c) and differ only on (b); 1 and 3 share (c), the property that makes both feed a downstream estimator. Nor are they mutually exclusive in practice: Mechanisms 3 and 5 co-occur inside a single file of a single audited repository, which is how we found Mechanism 5 at all. There is no partition criterion under which these are five disjoint kinds. That is not a weakness for the taxonomy's intended use — the axes are what tell an auditor *where to look* — but it does bear on what a mechanism-level severity comparison could mean: quantities overlapping on two of three axes are not five separate populations, so "which mechanism" is partly a question about position in this space rather than about kind.

**Their measured severities also cluster** (Table~): four differently-controlled point estimates — Delta_sel (Mechanism 2), Delta_synth and Delta_real (Mechanism 3's two harnesses) and Delta_boot (Mechanism 4) — happen to occupy a numerical span of +0.0011 to +0.0255 AUROC across four structurally unrelated leakage surfaces in two independent published pipelines. We report this as the range these four numbers occupy, *not* as a range over the five-mechanism taxonomy: each is stated against its own named control (Table~) rather than a shared one, and Mechanism 1 — the full-dataset-probe-reused-as-a-feature mechanism — is excluded from this span and measured on a visibly different scale: a new controlled reconstruction of the general mechanism (§4.1, `code/86`) gives BCa-established gaps of +0.13 to +0.29 AUROC, an order of magnitude above the four estimates above, while HaRP's own +0.19 narrative figure remains neither code-verified nor directly comparable to either set of numbers and is excluded from both (§3, §7). With that scoping stated, the four Mechanism 2–4 estimates are still small, and none separated from the others by anything like the margin an "it depends which leak you have" framing would need. **A caveat on the word "narrow."** On a ratio scale +0.0011 to +0.0255 is a 23x span, which is not narrow. The claim is on the *absolute* scale that matters for the practical question — whether a reader should expect a leak of this kind to move a reported AUROC by hundredths or by tenths — and the answer is hundredths for Mechanisms 2–4. Mechanism 1 is the exception (§4.1): the answer there is tenths. Where this paper uses ratios (§5.2–§5.4) it is comparing one quantity to itself under a controlled change, which is a different question; we flag the two scales explicitly to keep them from being conflated.

Clustering on the absolute scale is compatible with any of the three candidate axes being dominant on the ratio scale, so it settles nothing about which one matters. Table~ puts all three on one scale as the ratio between each axis's largest and smallest cell with the others held fixed (`code/72`). **Raw span** is the bare max/min; **denominator-sound span** restricts each axis to cells whose own BCa interval excludes zero, the only version §5.3's standard permits quoting, since every AUROC_0=0.985 cell and the low-contrast K=15 cells have intervals reaching zero and the two raw spans that use them therefore have no finite upper confidence limit — precisely the defect for which §5.3 withdraws the "48.6x". **On the sound comparison the mechanism-and-harness axis (9.2–24.3x) overlaps the operating-point axis (2.2–14.3x) rather than sitting cleanly above or below it**, and candidate count is the smallest of the three. ### 5.3 Two continuous quantities move severity within a fixed harness

`code/47` varies, one at a time and with the leakage mechanism held fixed, the number of candidates selected among (K), the selection-set size (n_val), and the operating point (the calibrated Bayes-optimal AUROC). **What survives, stated first.** Severity rises with K — monotonically and with diminishing returns — but the functional form of that K-regularity is *not* identified, and the regularity does not reproduce for exchangeable candidates: it is a property of checkpoint-along-a-trajectory selection, the thing this sweep actually varies, and every downstream use of it to reason about layer, threshold or hyperparameter selection is an extrapolation across candidate types this measurement does not support. The operating point moves severity far more.

**Candidate count.** Sweeping Kin1,...,225 moves the gap monotonically from +0.0000 to +0.0052. The fit is taken over the non-degenerate cells only (K>=15): at K=1, 3, 5 and 10 the LEAKY and CLEAN_MATCHED models come out bitwise identical in 100%/97%/90%/65% of (seed, fold) pairs, because too few epochs elapse for the two selection rules to land on different epochs, so those cells' near-zero gaps are a construction artifact rather than a measurement. The gate used is the one `code/57` already ships for the joint grid (bitwise-identical fraction <=0.50), read at runtime rather than re-chosen here; no cell is near it, retained cells sitting at <=0.36 and excluded ones at >=0.645. On the six surviving cells the gap is **monotone non-decreasing and concave in K** — strictly so at every step, in both cases — with an a+bln K slope of b=+0.00110 (95% CI [+0.00040,+0.00180], seed-level bootstrap; intercept a=-0.00042) — **scoped to selection among checkpoints along a training trajectory specifically**; the qualifier is not optional, see below. Including the degenerate cells changes the apparent dynamic range but not the slope: fitting all nine K>1 cells gives R^2=0.970 with b=0.00104, essentially unchanged (+6%), while the gap's span over the fitted cells moves from 2.31x (+0.00226 to +0.00522) to 6.10x — so most of that larger range was supplied by cells with no contrast to measure.

**The functional form is not identified, and we state the relationship as monotone-and-concave rather than as a logarithmic law.** R^2=0.939 for gap=a+bln K is not evidence the form is established: with per-seed data now shipped (`code/63`) the Monte-Carlo standard error of these six cell means averages 7.72x10^-4 against an RMS fit residual of 2.63x10^-4, so the noise on the points is 2.9x the misfit and that R^2 measures how monotone the cells are, not how logarithmic. Four other two-parameter concave forms beat ln K simultaneously on R^2, AICc and leave-one-out — a saturating exponential (R^2=0.9914), a+b/K (0.9787), a+bln ln K (0.9757) and a+bsqrt(ln K) (0.9595) — and a parametric bootstrap propagating each cell's own Monte-Carlo error puts P(ln K best) at 0.063 (`code/68`, Appendix~). This matters operationally: ln K implies unbounded growth in candidate count while the two best-fitting alternatives imply a ceiling, which is the opposite advice for an auditor asking whether a large candidate budget is a problem. What survives is what all the surviving forms agree on: severity rises with K, with diminishing returns.

**The sweep's achieved operating point drifts, and we disclose the confound rather than qualify the result.** It runs from 0.6269 at K=1 to 0.7574 at K=225, so the full-range fit is partly confounded with the operating-point relationship below — but the regularity survives restriction to K>=10, where that drift spans only 0.7565–0.7616 (b=0.00107, R^2=0.955, against b=0.00104, R^2=0.970 over the full contaminated range). That restriction and the degeneracy gate are separate and nearly coincident, agreeing on everything except K=10, which the gate excludes at 65% identical folds and the reported fit therefore drops.

**The candidates counted by K here are epochs of one training run, which is a narrower object than the paper had been using it as.** In `code/47`, K is literally `EPOCHS`: checkpoints along a single optimizer trajectory, which is not exchangeable — it is a learning curve dominated by the systematic climb from initialization to convergence — whereas §4.2, §4.4 and §4.5 reason about selection among 32 layers, 33 layers and 81 thresholds, all far closer to exchangeable. `code/73` re-runs Sweep A with exactly one substitution: K indexes K *independently seeded probes, each trained to the same fixed epoch count* (45), selected among by argmax, with generator, architecture, fold structure, arm construction and downstream readout imported unchanged. **The downstream relationship does not reproduce, at adequate power to say so with confidence.** At N_SEEDS=200 — this paper's own usual convention, and Sweep A's own seed count — the minimum detectable gap at 80% power is +0.00406, at or below the +0.00416 target effect (code/47's own K=45 epoch gap); a smaller, compute-limited pass at N_SEEDS=30 found the same qualitative pattern but was underpowered against this same target (Appendix~, item E38). At K=2,5,15,45 the gaps are +0.0012, -0.0008, -0.0009 and -0.0004; every BCa interval still contains zero (p=0.531 to 0.847), the sequence is neither monotone nor concave, and the a+bln K slope is b=-0.00043 (95% CI [-0.00173,+0.00088]) against Sweep A's b=+0.00110 (95% CI [+0.00040,+0.00180]) — wrong-signed, though the two intervals overlap enough that this comparison alone is not decisive; the per-cell comparison is sharper, since code/47's own K=15 and K=45 epoch-gap values fall *outside* this sweep's 95% interval at the matching cell. **The K-regularity measured over epochs of one training run does not reproduce for exchangeable candidates.**

**The null is not a failure of the harness to generate selection noise, and we checked that rather than assuming it.** Decomposing at the selection stage — the mean of (max - mean) over the K candidates' *selection-set* AUROCs, divided by sigma, against the exact mathbbE[max of K standard normals] — gives observed/exact-Gaussian ratios of 1.000, 1.003, 1.009 and 0.992 at K=2,5,15,45 (all within 1% of the extreme-value prediction, at N_SEEDS=200). **The winner's curse is present at the selection stage and scales with K exactly as extreme-value theory predicts**; what is absent is its *propagation* to the reported test AUROC once the selected model's features pass through fold reuse and a logistic readout. That is more specific and more useful than a bare null: the selection-stage curse and the reported-metric severity are different quantities, and this harness shows the first without the second. This design also supplies the sampling-noise SD the extreme-value form requires, which the trajectory sweep could not: a bias-corrected estimate of that SD (dividing by each cell's own c_4(K) correction, since a sample SD of K values is itself K-dependent) is nearly flat in K (range ratio 1.02), markedly flatter than the trajectory sweep's own dispersion (range ratio 2.66). The extreme-value form is therefore supported where it applies, at the selection stage, and is not a predictor of reported-metric severity here; in the trajectory sweep it remains **untestable as instrumented** rather than falsified, the only sigma available there being one training trajectory's per-epoch dispersion.

**A plausible mechanistic reading, which this design does not test and which should not be asserted beyond a hypothesis.** The selection-stage curse transfers to the exchangeable design exactly; only its downstream propagation does not. The natural interpretation is that the epoch-indexed K-law is carried by the systematic learning-curve structure named above — the climb from initialization to convergence, not candidate multiplicity per se — rather than by anything intrinsic to having more candidates. This experiment was not built to test that hypothesis and does not establish it; it is recorded here as the most useful open question this result raises, not as a finding.

**What this means for how this paper's own headline slope should be read.** b=+0.00110 should be scoped to selection among checkpoints along a training trajectory, the object it was actually measured on (§4.2's, §4.4's and §4.5's real candidate sets — 32 layers, 33 layers, 81 thresholds — are all closer to the exchangeable case tested here, where the downstream effect does not appear), and should not be extended, without this qualifier, to reason about the severity of selecting among exchangeable candidates in general. Full detail, the seed count and every per-cell value: see `results/exchangeable_candidate_sweep_n200.json` (`code/98`) and `EXTENDED_TECHNICAL_DETAIL.md`.

**Is K actually candidate count, or training length in disguise?** Sweep A trains a fresh model to exactly K total epochs for every K it reports, so "more candidates" and "more training" are the same manipulation and cannot be told apart from Sweep A alone. `code/88` decouples them directly: train *one* model to a fixed 225-epoch budget (Sweep A's own maximum) per seed, then vary *only* which of those 225 checkpoints are eligible for the argmax, by subsampling the candidate set at strides of 1, 3, 15 and 45 — effective candidate counts of 225, 75, 15 and 5, chosen to match four of Sweep A's own K values exactly. This costs no additional training beyond the two 225-epoch trajectories already needed (one for LEAKY's reused-fold selection statistic, one for the honest arm's disjoint carve-out), plus one cheap fixed-epoch retrain per stride for the honest arm — the same per-cell cost Sweep A already pays for every K it reports. Under a simplified single-fold, single-split readout (disclosed in `code/88`'s docstring; the same simplification used below for the non-Gaussian check), the decoupled gaps at K_eff=5, 15, 75, 225 are +0.0010, +0.0038, +0.0059 and +0.0064 — monotone non-decreasing, ln K slope +0.0014 (95% CI excludes zero only at the two largest cells, p=0.009 and p=0.010; p=0.064 and p=0.169 at 15 and 5) — while an internally-matched coupled baseline under the identical simplified harness (train-to-K-epochs directly, the structurally-coupled design) gives +0.0011, +0.0031, +0.0061 and +0.0063 at the same four nominal K, also monotone. **The K-regularity survives decoupling from training length**: candidate count, not training duration, is what drives it, at least for checkpoint-along-a-trajectory candidates under this simplified harness. This is a positive result for \S5.3's headline rather than a qualification of it, and we report it as such precisely because the opposite finding was equally available and would have forced a materially different conclusion.

**We state the power limitation as loudly as the result.** The exchangeable sweep ran at 30 seeds against Sweep A's 200, because it trains 2K+1 models per (seed, fold). Its mean per-cell SEM is 0.0035, a minimum detectable gap of +0.0099 at 80% power — *larger than the +0.0042 Sweep A measures at K=45* — and its slope interval [-0.0037,+0.0033] overlaps Sweep A's [+0.00040,+0.00180]. **This is a failure to reproduce at inadequate power, not a demonstrated contradiction, and it must not be read as one.** Three things keep it informative: all four cells fall on the *negative* side; at K=45 Sweep A's +0.0042 lies outside this sweep's [-0.0074,+0.0032]; and the one mechanism that could manufacture a spurious null — both honest rules picking the same candidate, forcing that fold's gap to zero — occurs at rate 1/K, negligible at K=45 (0.7%).

**Operating point.** This is the largest effect measured *inside this harness*. Holding the mechanism, K and n_val fixed and varying only the calibrated operating point, the gap falls from +0.0093 at AUROC_0=0.70 to +0.0036/+0.0011/+0.0007 at 0.80/0.90/0.95 and to +0.0002 at 0.985 — **a monotone decline to a level indistinguishable from zero** (p=0.219, n=100). **We report no multiplier for it.** A ratio of means has an unbounded confidence set exactly when Fieller's g=(t_.975 SE_denom/denom)^2>=1; regenerating both endpoint cells at per-seed resolution (`code/60`, reproducing the shipped JSON exactly) gives g=2.03, and g=1.93 from the shipped BCa halfwidth. A 200,000-draw paired bootstrap confirms it: 8.1% of resamples place the denominator at or below zero and the percentile interval of the ratio is [-340,+448], spanning zero, both signs and infinity; the denominator cell's own sign test is a coin flip (p=0.919; 49 positive, 47 negative, 4 exactly zero) and its per-seed SD is 7.2x its mean. The scientific content — a monotone decline to the floor — is unaffected; only the multiplier is withdrawn. The decline also does not transport outside the harness that produced it (§5.4).

**A variance-compression control, and a split verdict.** As AUROC approaches 1 the sampling variance of any AUROC estimate collapses against the bound, so *every* difference of AUROCs must shrink near the ceiling whether or not the leakage changes; on that account the decline is a property of the metric, and §5.5's exponential-in-probit form is what it predicts. `code/69` tests it on the per-seed Sweep C arms, re-expressing the same 100 paired contrasts per cell in three stabilized coordinates chosen to be different in kind: the *probit* difference, this harness's own binormal discriminability scale, in which a unit step means the same thing at 0.70 and 0.985; *paired Cohen's d*, which divides by precisely the dispersion the compression account says is shrinking; and a *sign statistic*, (n_+-n_-)/(n_++n_-), which uses only sign structure and is therefore invariant to *any* monotone reparameterization of AUROC. *The decline is real:* all three fall several-fold from 0.70 to 0.985 — probit +0.0246->+0.0034 (7.2x), Cohen's d +0.375->+0.139 (2.7x), sign statistic +0.313->+0.021 (15.0x) — and a pure compression account predicts mean and seed-to-seed SD fall at the *same* rate, leaving the standardized effect flat, whereas the mean's decline outruns the SD's by a factor of 2.7. *But most of the raw decline's size is coordinate effect:* on a log scale the share surviving stabilization is 0.51 in probit units, 0.70 on the sign measure and only 0.25 standardized. And *strict monotonicity does not survive*: in every stabilized coordinate the 0.95 cell sits at or slightly above the 0.90 cell. We therefore claim the operating point is a genuine severity modifier and *not* that the raw AUROC decline measures its size — a second, independent reason alongside Fieller's condition not to quote a multiplier (Appendix~).

**Selection-set size.** What we call the *sample-size* sweep varies N_SAMPLES, which moves training, test *and* per-fold validation sets together, not n_val alone; we name it accordingly and state the confound. The isolated version holds N_SAMPLES=700 fixed and varies only the inner CV fold count, which is what sets n_val (Sweep D), and gives a *monotone non-increasing* relationship in the direction *opposite* to the winner's-curse prediction: +0.0087 at n_val=280, +0.0036 at 186 and again at 112 (tied to within 3x10^-5), and +0.0005 (p=0.22) at 56. It is not that the selection signal fails to get noisier — the measured sigma_val moves as the theory expects; the gap simply does not follow it. What it follows is the operating point, which rises monotonically across exactly these cells because a smaller n_val means a larger per-fold training set. That residual coupling is inherent to cross-validation and we disclose it rather than claim complete isolation, but it makes this sweep further evidence for the operating-point account and against the 1/sqrtn_val one.

**Two qualifications on the size of the failure, both from §5.2 and both of which cut against us.** Matching the operating point on the control arm rather than the leaky one — neither convention is uniquely correct, and the shipped one maximizes the ratio — gives 13.1–28.7x instead of 15.1–42.0x on the shipped numerators, and 8.9–20.8x instead of 9.8–26.6x on the corrected ones (`code/84`): the control-arm convention lowers the ratio under either numerator, so it does not rescue the claim. And the upper end is capacity-specific: at capacity 384 the fidelity extension's ratio is 8.0x shipped (6.7x fold-matched), below the operating-point axis's own sound maximum either way. **The transport failure is therefore an order-of-magnitude effect on two observations, not a fixed 14–38x range**, and §5.2's magnitude comparison is scoped accordingly.

**What we therefore claim, and what we withdraw.** The operating-point decline is a property of the synthetic harness that produced it — its isotropic generative process, its single fixed leakage mechanic — and is *not* a severity law that transports across harnesses or across mechanisms at a matched operating point. The claim that "that single relationship moves severity more than any difference we measure between mechanisms" is withdrawn: the table above is precisely a difference between mechanisms and harnesses, at a matched operating point, larger than the relationship predicts by roughly an order of magnitude on every matching convention. What survives, and what we do claim, is weaker and more useful: **the operating point is a strong severity modifier *within* a harness, and two severity numbers measured at different operating points are not comparable until it is matched.** That supports this paper's actual methodological use of the relationship — explaining why ceiling-saturated cells show near-zero severity, and forbidding the direct synthetic-versus-real magnitude comparison we already decline to make (§4.3) — and not the prediction of any pipeline's severity from its operating point alone. Note also that the three rows differ in mechanic as well as harness, so this comparison does not separate the two; that is itself the point, since the withdrawn claim was that the operating point dominates mechanism differences.

### 5.5 Do the two axes combine into one function? An empirical joint fit

A joint function phi(K, n_sel, AUROC_0) upper-bounding the expected inflation would unify the five mechanisms and turn §6's checklist into a calculator; we attempt it here, and are explicit that what follows is an **empirical fit, not a bound and not a theorem**. The existing sweeps cannot answer it: Sweep A varies K at a fixed AUROC_0=0.80 and Sweep C varies AUROC_0 at a fixed K=45, so together they form a *cross* through the plane rather than a grid, and no cross can exhibit an interaction. `code/57` runs the missing cells: a full Kin15,45,135,405xAUROC_0in0.70,0.80,0.90,0.95,0.985 factorial in the same harness, n=100 seeds per cell, n_val held fixed at 112 throughout (Sweep D varies n_val in isolation and finds the gap moves *opposite* to the 1/sqrtn_val prediction, tracking the operating point instead, which is why we hold it fixed rather than folding it in as a third axis — a limitation of this surface, not a demonstration that n_val does not matter). The K=45 column reproduces Sweep C exactly.

**The grid excludes K=3, a degenerate cell.** At K=3 the two arms are bitwise identical in 81–100% of folds, so the gap there is 0.00000 by construction rather than by measurement; the grid instead spans Kin15,45,135,405, every cell carries a degeneracy record with the fit gated on it, and every conclusion below is refit five ways with each K column deleted in turn (Appendix~C.1, which also gives the per-column degeneracy fractions — these decay with K rather than switching off, so the gate is not a clean bill of health). An earlier version of this grid included K=3 and reported a significant interaction term on that basis (F(1,16)=5.92, p=0.027), entirely attributable to the degenerate cell; that claim is withdrawn (below) and Appendix~ records the correction.

**What this says.** A single joint model does describe this surface well, and it is the multiplicative one: severity factors, to a good approximation, into a candidate-count term and an operating-point term multiplied together. That form is *separable* — it contains no interaction term — so its advantage over the additive model is a statement about the *scale* on which the two axes combine, not evidence that they interact. This conclusion is robust in a way the withdrawn one was not: deleting each K column in turn leaves the interaction non-significant every time and the multiplicative fit strong throughout, with a stable operating-point coefficient and an unstable candidate-count exponent (Appendix~C.2).

**This forces a qualification of one of this paper's own headline numbers.** The K-relationship of §5.3 is measured along a single row of this grid, at AUROC_0=0.80. Across the grid as a whole ln K *alone* explains essentially none of the variance (R^2=0.038, and at LOO -0.192 worse than predicting the mean), so the K-regularity is real *within* an operating point and not a description of severity in general. Two qualifications on the K-spans in the table: the span is not monotone in headroom (it peaks in the middle, at 0.90, and is *smallest* at 0.70, the row with the most headroom), and all three of the largest are set at their low end by the K=15 column, which carries the least contrast in this grid (24–42% bitwise-identical folds, worst exactly at the cell producing the 7.1x); excluding that column the spans collapse to 1.1–1.4x. What survives is the qualitative point and not the multiplier: candidate count moves the gap where there is headroom and not at AUROC_0=0.985. This qualification is *stronger* on the corrected grid than on the degenerate one it replaces (R^2=0.038 against 0.114), so unlike the interaction claim it does not depend on the removed cells.

**What this fit does not license.** It is a surface fit inside one synthetic harness with one mechanism held fixed at one fixed n_val, and it is an average, not an upper bound, so it cannot certify that any particular pipeline's inflation is below anything. It also does not transport across harnesses: §5.4 shows two real-feature harnesses exceeding this surface's own operating-point relationship by roughly an order of magnitude at a matched operating point. We report it as a useful empirical description and name a formally-derived bound as future work. The out-of-sample check against the five held-out Sweep A cells does not currently discriminate between models — two of those cells are themselves degenerate and Sweep A runs 200 seeds against this grid's 100 — so we read nothing into its ordering, and this harness does not support an out-of-sample test of the joint surface (`EXTENDED_TECHNICAL_DETAIL.md`).

### 5.7 Does this depend on normality? A non-Gaussian generative process

\S5's operating-point coordinate, z_0=Phi^-1(AUROC_0), is calibrated via the closed-form binormal identity throughout \S5, for isotropic and (in \S4.3's anisotropic sweep) Gaussian features; anisotropy is relaxed elsewhere in this paper, but normality itself never is. `code/92` adds one non-Gaussian generative process — a two-component mixture of Gaussians per class (a bimodal sub-population within each class, e.g. "easy" and "hard" cases with different feature geometry), calibrated to the same target AUROC_0 values via Monte Carlo bisection on the class-mean separation (achieved AUROC of the signal-axis projection, over 60,000-draw samples, matched to within 0.001) rather than the closed-form binormal identity, which does not apply to a mixture — and reruns the operating-point sweep under it, holding capacity, epochs and sample size fixed at this paper's own values. A simplified single-fold, single-split harness is used throughout (disclosed above, \S5.3), with an isotropic-Gaussian control run under the *identical* simplified harness so the comparison is internal rather than against Sweep C's own absolute numbers.

| Generative process | AUROC_0=0.70 | 0.80 | 0.90 | 0.95 | 0.985 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-Gaussian mixture | +0.0102 | +0.0025 | +0.0038 | +0.0014 | +0.0011 |
| Isotropic-Gaussian control (identical harness) | +0.0054 | +0.0056 | +0.0025 | +0.0008 | +0.0009 |

Both decline by an order of magnitude from the lowest to the highest operating point, and neither is strictly monotone: the mixture's 0.80->0.90 step rises rather than falls, and so does the Gaussian control's own 0.70->0.80 step — this simplified harness's own Gaussian baseline already fails strict monotonicity at this seed count, consistent with §5.3's finding that it does not survive variance-stabilization in the primary harness either. The honest reading is a qualified positive: the large-scale, order-of-magnitude decline with operating point survives under this non-Gaussian process, at a similar magnitude to the Gaussian control measured the same way, so the relationship's broad shape does not depend on Gaussianity specifically — but neither harness establishes exact monotone decline at every intermediate point, so that finer claim is not made here or in §5.3.

### 5.8 Why this matters practically, and what it does not establish

The operating-point relationship explains, without appeal to mechanism identity, why several severity estimates here come out near zero: they were measured on tasks already running at AUROC >=0.98, where there is almost no headroom for selection to inflate anything. That includes the 12 ceiling-saturated cells of Case Study 4 (+0.0016 against +0.0026 on the non-saturated cells under Delta_boot, and 1.5 to 5.5x across the three estimators considered there), MultiHaluDet's own 0.985 regime, and this paper's own real-feature harness before it was recalibrated. A reader auditing a pipeline should therefore ask *how many candidates were tried* and *where on the operating-point range the reported number sits* before comparing any two severity figures — and should not read that as licence to stop asking *which* of the five mechanisms is present, since §5.2's comparison puts the mechanism-and-harness axis at or above both.

**What this does not establish.** The three sweeps vary one quantity at a time and the joint grid varies two, all within a single synthetic harness holding the mechanism fixed; none is a matched head-to-head across all five mechanisms at common (K, n_val, AUROC_0), which is what would test whether mechanism identity explains any *residual* variance once these are controlled. We do not claim it explains none. §5.4 sharpens what that missing experiment has to be: two of its three harnesses differ in mechanic as well as in harness and exceed the synthetic surface's prediction by more than an order of magnitude at a matched operating point, which is not evidence that mechanism identity matters — harness and mechanic are confounded in exactly those rows — but does mean the one-quantity-at-a-time result cannot be read as showing mechanism identity unimportant *in general*, only unimportant relative to K and the operating point *within* a single harness. Disentangling the two is the highest-value experiment this line of work has open. \S reports it.

### 5.9 A matched head-to-head across all five mechanisms: does identity matter beyond K and the operating point? A richer, per-mechanism model fits decisively better, within these harnesses — whether that reflects mechanism identity or the harnesses' unequal maturity is not yet separated.

Every prior subsection here varies one or two quantities *within one mechanism's* harness. This one holds K and AUROC_0 fixed across mechanisms instead: a shared grid, Kin5,10,20,40,80x AUROC_0in0.70,0.80,0.95 (15 cells), realized separately inside a synthetic reconstruction of each mechanism (`code/97`), so that any remaining difference in severity is attributable to mechanism identity rather than to K or the operating point differing across mechanisms as a confound. Mechanisms 1–4 are measured on the AUROC scale (n=48 cells: Mechanism 1 supplies 3 cells, one per AUROC_0, since it has no candidate count to sweep — see below — and Mechanisms 2–4 supply 15 each); Mechanism 5 is analyzed separately on the F1 scale, per §2's own metric-relativity principle, since its AUROC gap is exactly zero by construction at every cell and cannot be pooled with an AUROC-scale comparison.

**The harnesses are not of equal maturity, and we say so before giving the result.** Mechanisms 1, 4 and 5 reuse or directly adapt this paper's own already-multiply-corrected machinery (`code/86`, `code/45`'s non-negative Delta_boot estimator, and `code/46`'s threshold-selection harness, respectively). Mechanism 3 reuses `code/73`'s exchangeable-candidate design but at only N_SEEDS=15 (compute-limited; mean per-cell SEM 0.00514, minimum detectable gap at 80% power +0.0144 — most cells are individually indistinguishable from zero at this seed count, consistent with `code/73`'s own prior finding, and its intercept below should be read with this in mind). **Mechanism 2 required a wholly new synthetic reconstruction — no prior version existed anywhere in this codebase.** It represents K exchangeable candidate "layers" as independent isotropic-Gaussian feature blocks of the same samples, each calibrated to the same target AUROC_0, with severity defined identically in form to GUARDIAN's own Delta_sel (§4.2): the selected candidate's cross-validated AUROC minus the mean of all candidates' held-out AUROCs. Unlike this paper's other harnesses, it has been through none of the multiple rounds of adversarial correction the rest of §4 and §5 have.

**Two nested models, fit to the pooled 48 AUROC-scale cells – and, in the same breath, what this comparison does and does not establish.** A mechanism-agnostic joint model of exactly §5.5's form, gap=exp(a+betaln K+cz_0), gets R^2=0.826 (LOO 0.807). Allowing one intercept per mechanism, gap=exp(a_m+betaln K+cz_0), gets R^2=0.997 (LOO 0.986), and a nested F-test rejects the pooled model decisively: F(3,42)=799.7, p=1.1x10^-16 (DeltaAICc=-187.5 in the mechanism-aware model's favour) – but this model comparison establishes only that some factor beyond K and the operating point matters, not that the factor is mechanism identity itself rather than the unequal harness maturity/power correlated with it (Mechanism 2 newly built and, until the check reported below, never independently stress-tested; Mechanism 3 underpowered at N_SEEDS=15) – see below for that check and for what would still be needed to separate the two fully. The nested F-test's degrees-of-freedom accounting also assumes the 48 cells are independent; cells within a mechanism share generator code and, in several cases, a pooled data structure (§4.3), which could correlate their residuals and inflate the test's nominal significance. We have not run a clustered-residual correction for this specific test (§4.4's own 24-cell aggregate uses a dataset-clustered interval for a structurally similar concern, 20% wider than the naive one); given the per-mechanism intercept spread below is 304x, we judge it very unlikely that such a correction would overturn the qualitative conclusion, but we have not verified this directly and flag it as an open technical question rather than a closed one. The four per-mechanism intercepts span a 304x multiplicative range on the raw scale (log-scale range 5.72, SD 2.37).

**A second, mechanistic demonstration of the same point, not just an AICc number.** The pooled model's own ln K coefficient comes out wrong-signed, beta=-1.42 — directly contradicting this paper's own established K-law (§5.3, b>0) — purely as a compositional artifact: Mechanism 1 sits at fixed ln K=0 with a large gap while Mechanisms 2–4 sit at larger K with small gaps, and with no mechanism term to absorb that scale difference, the pooled fit reads the across-mechanism contrast as a within-mechanism trend. Once mechanism identity is controlled for, the shared ln K coefficient flips to correctly signed, beta=+0.255, alongside a correctly-signed operating-point term c=-0.733. A reader who pooled severities across mechanisms without a mechanism term here would not just lose precision; they would draw the wrong-signed conclusion about candidate count itself.

**Matched-cell comparison, descriptively.** Restricting to the 6 of 15 (K,AUROC_0) cells where Mechanisms 2, 3 and 4's raw gaps are all positive, the max/min ratio among the three ranges 6.8–207x (median 9.9x) at identical (K,AUROC_0) — not close, by any reading.

**On the F1 scale, Mechanism 5 alone**, the same log-linear form fits far more weakly (R^2=0.372) and is not pooled with the AUROC-scale mechanisms above; it is reported here only for completeness, not as part of the identity-versus-covariates comparison.

**What this does and does not establish.** This narrows, but does not fully close, this paper's own largest open question: at matched (K,AUROC_0), some factor beyond K and the operating point carries information a two-covariate model cannot recover, in these harnesses. It remains a controlled-harness result across five synthetic reconstructions of unequal maturity — Mechanism 2's newly built, Mechanism 3's underpowered at N_SEEDS=15, Mechanism 1 supplying no K-slope information at all — and this comparison's own design cannot distinguish that factor being mechanism identity from it being harness maturity or power correlated with mechanism identity: the two least-mature harnesses are exactly the ones whose intercepts would need checking against an independently-built second construction to tell the two apart. That check has now been run for Mechanism 2 (below) and found stable; Mechanism 3's underpowering remains unaddressed by a second construction. Nor is this a claim about the five real audited pipelines at a matched operating point, which §5.4 already shows do not transport onto this paper's synthetic relationship by an order of magnitude. Nine of the 48 cells had a raw gap <=0 and were floored to 10^-6 so the multiplicative fit is defined; this does not change which model wins. Full per-cell values, harness designs and fit diagnostics are in `results/matched_head_to_head_five_mechanisms.json` (`code/97`); the run took 10.0 hours of wall time under heavy, unrelated concurrent CPU load, not because the computation itself is expensive (it is CPU-only synthetic simulation).

**An independent second construction of Mechanism 2, run to test exactly this.** Construction A represents the K candidate "layers" as independent isotropic-Gaussian draws (`code/97`). A genuinely different construction (`code/99`) changes exactly one thing: independence across candidates is replaced with a one-factor latent model, X_k=class_sep/2*sign+sqrt(rho) L+sqrt(1-rho) E_k, where L is one latent draw shared by all K candidates for that seed and rho=0.5 is disclosed and fixed before the run — so adjacent candidates carry correlated signal, the property real transformer layers have and that construction A's design assumes away, while each candidate's own marginal distribution, and hence its calibration to the target AUROC_0, is unchanged. Run at the identical grid and N_SEEDS=100: construction B's own fitted intercept is a_B=-3.349 against construction A's a_2=-3.389 (Delta=0.040, well inside the pre-registered stability threshold of one order of magnitude, ln 10~2.303); the two constructions' 15 matched per-cell gaps correlate at Pearson r=0.982 (p=9.3x10^-11); and substituting construction B for construction A inside the full four-mechanism pooled comparison shifts Mechanism 2's fitted intercept by only +0.035 (to -3.353) while leaving the qualitative result intact (R^2 0.851->0.997, nested F(3,42)=658.7, p=1.1x10^-16, intercept spread 271x against the original 304x). By the rule fixed before this check was run, Mechanism 2's intercept is **STABLE** across two constructions differing in exactly the property — cross- candidate correlation — most likely to matter for an argmax-over-K selection rule. **This is one robustness check on one of the two least-mature harnesses, not a general resolution**: it leaves Mechanism 3's underpowering untouched, it tests one alternative construction rather than sweeping the correlation continuously or varying the estimator family, and a stable intercept under this specific perturbation is evidence against, not proof against, the harness-maturity confound the reviewers named. Taken at face value, it is evidence that Mechanism 2's fitted severity level is not an artifact of its particular independence assumption — the most natural first alternative construction to have checked it against — which is a genuinely good-faith result to report regardless of which way it had come out: an unstable intercept would have confirmed the confound directly; a stable one narrows it by exactly this much and no further. Full detail is in `results/mechanism2_construction_b_correlated_latent.json` (`code/99`).

## 6. A Checklist for This Literature

[Full version in `draft/leakage_checklist.md`.] Five questions correspond to the five mechanisms above:

1. sep0pt

2. Is any feature computed by fitting something on the full dataset's labels before an outer CV loop treats part of that dataset as held out?

3. Inside a CV fold, is a "best" checkpoint/epoch chosen using the same fold whose features/predictions get reused downstream as clean OOF output?

4. Is a "best" layer/config selected by the argmax of a metric computed on the same test set later reported as the headline number, across many candidates, with no correction?

5. Was a hyperparameter chosen by maximizing a cross-validated metric, with that same CV number then reported as performance, with no further genuinely held-out check?

6. Was a decision threshold or other operating point chosen by optimizing a metric on the test labels themselves, with that same test set then used to report the metric at that threshold?

The item-by-item comparison against Kapoor & Narayanan's eight-type taxonomy, REFORMS' three leakage questions and the NeurIPS ML Reproducibility Checklist is in `EXTENDED_TECHNICAL_DETAIL.md` in the supplementary material, together with the blinded second-rater protocol and the scanner's two failure causes in full.

### 6.2 We tried to automate this checklist; the attempt itself is informative

We built a regex-based scanner for the original four patterns (Mechanism 5 was found after it was built and is not covered) and ran it against 7 repositories: the two already-audited case studies, to test whether it catches known bugs, plus 5 not-previously-examined, externally-found hidden-state-probing repositories, to test its false-positive rate on presumed-clean pipelines (`code/04`). It is a heuristic line-matcher, not a validated static analyzer; every flag was manually read and classified before being treated as a finding.

**How the hits were classified, and the reliability limits of that classification.** The labels were originally a single rater's manual reading — this paper's author, who already knew where both real bugs were: no inter-rater reliability, no blinding, an interested rater, and none of it disclosed. We obtained an independent second reading of the same 7 sites under a protocol withholding this paper's draft, its checklist and the location of the known bugs. **That second rater was a language model, not a second human**, which bounds what the agreement is worth: it checks that the classification survives a reader with no stake in the result and no knowledge of the answer, not that two domain experts converge. Agreement was 7 of 7 (all false positives on the same provenance argument), so percent agreement is 100% and *Cohen's kappa is undefined here* on a single-category marginal — a weak reliability check on seven non-independent sites from two repositories. The same blind read also located both known true positives unprompted (evidence the two false negatives below are the *scanner's* failures, not artifacts of privileged knowledge) and surfaced two further findings — a disagreement over whether MultiHaluDet's checkpoint selection qualifies (called "a mild stacking impurity"), and two further sites in `HallucinationPatternDetection` confirming Mechanism 5 is not specific to MultiHaluDet (§4.5) — neither of which changes a severity number or enters the 7-hit count. Full protocol: `EXTENDED_TECHNICAL_DETAIL.md`.

**The scanner does not work, for two specific reasons.** It produced 7 raw hits in 2 repos, and manual reading showed all 7 are false positives: 3 flag t-SNE/UMAP/PCA calls used only for plotting; 4 flag `haloscope`'s layer/threshold selection, where reading the code shows selection correctly uses a separate validation split — the opposite of leakage, and a positive data point for that pipeline (HaloScope; Du et al. 2024). More tellingly it missed both known true positives, and **both misses have identifiable causes in its own construction:** a plain keyword-list gap at Case Study 3's site (its `CHECKPOINT_KEYWORDS` list omits the `best_auc`/`best_model` tokens the audited trainer binds), and at Case Study 4's a design choice requiring an argmax token and a `test`-named variable on the same line and in the same file, which cannot fire there however the keyword lists are extended because the test split lives one call away in another module. Zero true positives across all 7 repos, and two false negatives on the two known ones. We deliberately do not read that as evidence that the leakage class is inherently resistant to static detection — the first cause needs a longer keyword list, the second a dataflow analysis rather than line matching. What the exercise establishes is narrower: *line-level regex matching* is the wrong tool here, because every one of these mechanisms is a relationship between a selection statement and the provenance of the quantity it selects on, and that relationship is routinely split across lines, functions and files.

This is a statement about 2 of 7 repositories found by targeted search for exactly this failure class, not a random or exhaustive sample (HaRP and GUARDIAN were never scanned at all). **On what a non-regex approach can do, and why we do not generalize from that either:** the blinded second-rater protocol is incidentally an instance of one, and it located both true positives plus the two additional sites the regex scanner missed — but that is one run, on two repositories already known to contain bugs, with no false-positive rate measured on the five presumed-clean ones. It is enough to say the failure above is *this scanner's* rather than automated detection's as a category, and nowhere near enough to say a language-model scanner works; measuring that properly, on a pre-registered corpus with both classes present, is follow-on work this paper does not do.

### 6.3 Validating the prescribed fixes, and what they cost

Each checklist item above carries a "Fix:" in its full version (`draft/leakage_checklist.md`), and until now none of them had been validated against a naive alternative or costed. `code/89` does both for two mechanisms whose fixes are already implemented elsewhere in this paper, making the validation explicit rather than leaving it implicit in already-shipped numbers.

**Mechanism 2's fix is nested cross-validation**: select using an inner CV score, report on a genuinely disjoint outer set, never using the selection statistic itself as the reported number. \S4.2's own design — `cv_auroc_selectpool` for selection, `heldout_auroc` for reporting, repeated over 50 randomized splits — already *is* this fix; it had simply never been framed against the naive alternative it replaces. Reconstructing that naive alternative directly from the shipped artifact (`results/case_study_2_probe_scores.npz`): for each of the 50 splits, reporting the CV-selected layer's own CV score (no held-out check at all — Cawley & Talbot's canonical mistake) gives a mean of 0.7740; this paper's already-shipped nested design gives 0.7508 at the same selected layers. **Nesting removes +0.0232 AUROC of optimism** (BCa 95% CI [+0.0082,+0.0383], p=0.0048), and the resulting nested estimate sits -0.0127 from a selection-independent reference (the modal selected layer's own mean held-out AUROC across all 50 splits, a quantity that involves no argmax and so cannot itself be optimistic) — a non-trivial residual, about half the removed gap, consistent with this paper's own decomposition (\S4.2: winner's-curse term +0.042865 minus transferred layer quality +0.0173, net Delta_sel=+0.0255): the selected layer does carry real signal, so the nested estimate is not expected to land exactly on a layer-agnostic reference. **Cost:** building the artifact this validation reuses required 32 layers x (5 inner-CV folds +1 full fit) x 52 split variants =9,984 `LogisticRegression` fits on already-extracted probe features — sub-second per fit on CPU, no GPU or hidden-state extraction; re-running the validation itself from the shipped artifact takes 3 seconds.

**Mechanism 3's fix is a genuinely disjoint held-out selection set**, which \S4.3's "fully corrected (out-of-fold)" arm already is. Re-verifying directly from the shipped JSONs (not re-trusting one prior print statement): on the synthetic harness the fix moves the gap from +0.0030 (shipped) to -0.0001 (fully corrected), a reduction of +0.0031 landing on a BCa interval that includes zero — **the fix removes the detectable synthetic effect entirely**, at a logged cost of 288 seconds (4.8 minutes) of CPU time (`code/77`'s own `runtime_seconds`). On real features it does not remove the effect entirely: residual gaps of +0.0006 (capacity 128) and +0.0030 (capacity 384) survive, at a logged cost of 3,166 seconds (52.8 minutes) (`code/78`+`79`'s combined runtime). **Both prescribed fixes work operationally, and both are cheap in absolute terms**: minutes of CPU time re-fitting probes or re-running a synthetic harness on already-extracted features or already-specified generative processes, no GPU and no new hidden-state extraction in either case — the concrete cost figure behind \S6's "cheap to fix" claim.

## 7. Discussion and Limitations

**Data and code availability.** All code, cached result JSONs and the paper source ship in the anonymized supplementary material, and will be released publicly upon publication. Case Studies 2, 3 and 4 are reproducible on the terms §3 grades them against; **Case Study 1 is the exception, and no script, log or data file supporting its +0.19 figure is included.** The §6 regex linter ships in full and is, per its own results, a confirmed non-detector on this corpus — included for transparency, not as a working tool.

**Scope, and what these numbers mean operationally.** This is an evaluation-protocol audit, not an inference-time method, so fixing any of the five mechanisms makes no claimed difference to deployment cost; the value is entirely in correcting reported detection metrics. The code-verified inflations measured here are on the order of 0.001 to 0.026 AUROC. Practitioners should read that as: these mechanisms are real and worth fixing, because they are cheap to fix and because they corrupt comparisons between methods, but not as an argument that the subfield's reported numbers are broadly wrong by large margins. Where large gaps *do* appear (Mechanism 5's +0.022 to +0.052), they are on threshold-dependent metrics, not AUROC. The audit's central lesson is one this paper evidences from its own record: acknowledging a confound is not a substitute for sizing it, and checking that two arms differ is not a substitute for verifying that they do. Of the sixteen corrections in Appendix~, the ones that moved a number most were those in which a confound already *acknowledged* in the text was subsequently *tested* — the fidelity extension's budget match resized a conclusion that had rested on a hedge, and §4.3's selection-budget factorial removed a headline estimate's claim to be a detectable effect at all while attributing 75.5% of it to a single construction choice. Symmetrically, an *untested* control can turn out to be an *identical* one: §4.3's adaptivity control was bitwise the same arm as the budget-deficit control it was contrasted with.

**Limitations.** Each is stated once here, with a pointer to where the full numbers already appear — they are not repeated in this list. *(1)* Case Study 1 is not re-verifiable from this artifact and its +0.19 is excluded from every quantitative claim (§3, §4.1). Mechanism 1's own controlled reconstruction (§4.1) is new and preliminary — a first pass, not yet iterated the way Mechanism 3's sixteen corrections have been, and architecturally unrelated to HaRP's own harness — so the abstract's and §5.1's four-estimate severity span excludes Mechanism 1 rather than bounding it: preliminary as the evidence is, it points to Mechanism 1 being the most severe of the five, not merely unmeasured. *(2)* §5's one-quantity-at-a-time sweeps and joint fit (§5.3–§5.5) hold the mechanism fixed within a single synthetic harness; the matched (K,AUROC_0) head-to-head that tests whether identity explains residual variance beyond that is now run (§5.9) and finds that it does, but only within that comparison's own scope — five controlled-harness reconstructions of unequal maturity, not the five real audited pipelines at a matched operating point, which §5.4 already shows do not transport onto this paper's synthetic relationship by an order of magnitude. *(2a)* We offer no formally-derived bound: §5.5's empirical fit is not a bound, and the adaptive-data-analysis literature (§2, §) gives a principled reason to expect concavity in ln K but not the operating-point term, since its bounds are worst-case and metric-agnostic while this effect is dominated by ceiling proximity; one specific obstacle is that this harness's isolated n_val dependence runs opposite to the 1/sqrtn_sel direction such a derivation would predict (§5.3). *(2b)* The operating-point axis's raw magnitude is partly the metric's own geometry (Appendix~), which with Fieller's condition is why we report it as a modifier and never a multiplier. *(2c)* Case Study 2's +0.0255 is a within-dataset figure, all reps resampling splits of the same 700 samples, read against a mechanical null rather than zero (§4.2). *(3)* The K-regularity is monotone and concave but its functional form is not identified, and the four best-fitting alternatives disagree with ln K about whether severity saturates — precisely the question an auditor with a large candidate budget is asking (§5.3, Appendix~). We have no mechanistic explanation for either property. *(4)* The "is it adaptivity itself?" alternative for Mechanism 3 is *untested*, not rejected: the control built to test it turned out bitwise identical to the budget-deficit control, and the only measurement bearing on it is the factorial's null depth factor, which varies *when* an arm stops rather than whether its stopping rule was adaptive (§4.3). The design that would settle it — an adaptive control matched to LEAKY on both budget and selection-set size, which cannot be built inside one fold — is not run. *(4a)* The fixed-d eigenspectrum experiment separates covariance shape from dimensionality but not from operating point, since anisotropy lowers the achieved operating point even at fixed J (§4.3). *(4b)* Mechanism 3's measured severity depends on a downstream ensembling step, but less than we reported: against a corrected control only the direct out-of-fold readout retains a gap, and this line is withdrawn from the evidence carrying the severity (§4.3). *(4c)* The fidelity extension's two arms differ in training depth as well as validation signal, reported rather than equalized because when to stop *is* the leaked decision, and we can no longer attribute its severity to coupling *continuity*, since an isolating single-argmax arm scores higher; separating coupling continuity from training depth is a missing experiment, and the extension's severity is itself strongly capacity-dependent (§4.3), which is why neither figure is quoted as a mechanism-level severity (§5.2). Mechanism 5's gaps are measured at n_test=140 and shrink at larger n (§4.5). *(4d)* The scanner's classification rests on two raters, not a validated protocol: the primary rater is this paper's author and knew the ground truth, and while a second, blinded rater agreed on every case, Cohen's kappa is undefined on a single-category marginal (§6.2). *(4e)* The out-of-fold control that removes Mechanism 3's synthetic effect has a limit of its own — it runs at a smaller effective training pool, on whose effect this paper's own sweeps disagree in sign — which is why §4.3 treats it alongside the fold-matched control as decisive enough to report and not decisive enough to retract the mechanism (§4.3). *(5)* We could not re-run either external paper's full 7B-scale pipeline within this paper's compute budget, so Case Studies 3 and 4's severity for the *actual published numbers* remains open. The real-feature test is single-dataset and single-language despite MultiHaluDet's own contribution being multilingual, and even leak-free, no calibration brings this feature set onto the synthetic sweep's operating point, so whether the synthetic estimates transfer at a matched operating point remains untested (§4.3). A second model family (§4.3.3) partially addresses the single-model-family gap but not the dataset or language one, and lands at an even higher operating point without a pinned model revision — one additional data point consistent with the operating-point account, not a general replication. *(6) This paper's own correction process is itself a source of researcher degrees of freedom, and we name it rather than leave it implicit.* Sixteen corrections behind §4.3 and four further rounds behind Case Studies 2 and 4, Mechanism 5 and the severity surface are each disclosed with their direction and magnitude, and most reversed or attenuated an earlier claim. But the sequence of which asymmetry to examine next was not itself pre-registered — only the 2x2x2 factorial's own decision rule was fixed before it ran. An iterative process that revisits its own prior conclusions in light of what earlier steps turned up can in principle be steered by how intermediate results look, even when every step is honestly reported and every number's provenance is traceable; we have no way to rule this out from inside the process that produced it. The numbers here should be read as the result of thorough, disclosed adversarial review, not as the output of a single protocol fixed in advance of seeing any data. *(7) Under family-wise error control, no marginal positive finding outside Mechanism 5 survives.* We state this consequence of our decision rule at the end of §3, where Table~ gives the Holm-Bonferroni verdict for every family checked: Holm eliminates the marginal finding everywhere except the anisotropic-covariance sweep's two capacity-128 cells (§4.3.4). *(8) Two alternative readings of this paper's own correction record are not otherwise discussed, and we name them.* **Residual asymmetry:** Table~'s correction sequence is monotone non-increasing across all four rows with no sign yet of convergence; we cannot rule out that a fourth correction round would drive the surviving real-feature severities to zero too, the way it did the primary synthetic sweep (§4.3.2). A follow-up check (§) narrows this worry without closing it: in an analytically-known-ground-truth reconstruction, the fully-corrected estimator is itself calibrated rather than biased toward zero, so the residual asymmetry is not an artifact of the correction procedure — but the toy model has no analogue of the real-feature confounds a fourth correction round could still uncover. **Fidelity gradient:** ordering this paper's three Mechanism-3 harnesses by how faithfully each reproduces the audited trainer, severity increases monotonically with fidelity (§5.4's transport-failure table) — the *opposite* direction from this paper's reassuring headline that severities are uniformly small. A reader who takes the fidelity gradient at face value should weight the more faithful harnesses' larger numbers over the synthetic sweep's smaller one, not the reverse.

## 8. Broader Impact and Ethical Considerations

This paper adds no detection capability; its subject is the trustworthiness of reported detector performance, so the foreseeable harm pathway runs through misplaced confidence rather than through misuse. A detector whose reported AUROC or F1 is inflated by any of the five mechanisms here will, at a fixed threshold, pass more hallucinations and flag more correct outputs than its published numbers promise, and a practitioner who carries such a number into a higher-stakes setting (clinical or legal document generation, automated monitoring of a model's factual claims) imports that gap into decisions where the cost of a missed hallucination and the cost of a false flag are not symmetric. **The taxonomy and checklist are a partial mitigation and explicitly not a guarantee, and this paper's own record is why we say so:** two of our own measurement instruments were found committing the exact pattern we audit for (§4.3), four rounds of adversarial review were each needed to remove a different confound from a single case study, and Mechanism 5 was found only after the checklist and its scanner had already been written and did not cover it. Working through §6 raises the chance a leak is caught; it does not establish that a pipeline is clean. The severity numbers cut the other way and deserve the same care: the Mechanism 2–4 estimates (Table~) land between roughly 0.000 and 0.026 AUROC — Mechanism 1 the exception, an order of magnitude higher (§4.1) — which is neither licence to ignore these mechanisms, since they are cheap to fix and corrupt method comparisons, nor a measurement of what they cost in any deployed system: these figures come from specific synthetic harnesses and two audited third-party pipelines, at operating points and test-set sizes we chose or inherited, and severity in a different system has to be measured there rather than read off this paper.

## 9. Conclusion

Five structurally distinct label-information-leakage mechanisms recur across hidden-state hallucination-detection pipelines, including our own; three are verified directly against pinned commits of externally published code. That taxonomy is this paper's most durable contribution, and we expect it to outlast every number in it.

**The severities are small on the AUROC scale for Mechanisms 2–4, and hard to establish; Mechanism 1 is not among them.** Table~ gives each code-verified estimate with its control, interval and verifiability grade; the four Mechanism 2–4 point estimates span roughly 0.000 to 0.026 AUROC, each against its own named control rather than one shared scale (§3), while test-set threshold selection leaves AUROC exactly unaffected by construction and yet inflates F1 and accuracy by +0.022 to +0.052 — a clear case that a pipeline can be simultaneously clean on one reported metric and leaking on another. Mechanism 1 is the exception: Case Study 1's +0.19 is reported from prior work and excluded from every comparison as unverifiable, but a new, code-verified synthetic reconstruction of its general mechanism (§4.1) measures a comparably large, BCa-established +0.13 to +0.29 AUROC, an order of magnitude above Mechanisms 2–4 and in the same tenths-of-AUROC range as HaRP's own figure — not independent corroboration of that specific number, which remains unverifiable, but reason to read Mechanism 1 as plausibly the most severe of the five rather than merely unmeasured – tempered by a real-feature check of the same mechanism (§4.1) finding a gap an order of magnitude smaller at a higher operating point, so this reading is not settled.

**Two of those numbers are as much results about measurement as about leakage.** Mechanism 3's *primary* synthetic estimate does not survive its own control: a 2x2x2 factorial crossing selection-set size, selection-run budget and training depth (§4.3) carries the shipped control's +0.0030 to a fully corrected -0.0001 (BCa [-0.0021,+0.0021], p=0.657), attributing 75.5% of the movement to selection-set size alone — the only one of the three shares a seed-level bootstrap distinguishes from zero. *The decomposition, not the null, is the contribution*: it names the construction choice that manufactures a false positive of this size and is reusable by anyone building a control of this kind. Mechanism 4's estimate rests on a statistic two theorems here prove can never be negative, which withdraws every inference drawn from its sign and moves that case study's weight onto a permutation null, against which no cell is significant *above* its own null (§4.4). Severity is a property of a (mechanism, metric, *control*) *triple*: on one Mechanism-3 cell, two defensible controls span 1.45x and a third we disqualify on independent grounds would make it 5.9x — the disqualification, a judgement about which counterfactual is admissible, is what fixes the number.

**What severity tracks within a harness, and what it tracks across harnesses.** Within one harness, severity rises monotonically and concavely with candidate count (§5.3) — robustly, though no specific functional form is identified, and scoped to the candidate type actually varied, since an exchangeable-candidate replication does not reproduce it downstream — and falls far more sharply with the operating point, to a level indistinguishable from zero at AUROC_0=0.985. The two axes are separable rather than interacting (§5.5) and neither is a law: the operating-point relationship does not transport out of its own harness, is exceeded by roughly an order of magnitude at a matched operating point in this paper's own real-feature harnesses (§5.4), and its raw magnitude is partly the metric's geometry. Placed on one scale (§5.2), that transport failure leaves the mechanism-and-harness axis overlapping the operating-point axis rather than below it, with candidate count smallest on every convention — licensing a negative conclusion, not a positive ranking: we cannot rule out mechanism identity contributing at least as much to severity as the operating point. **The matched head-to-head that would help settle this is now run** (§5.9): across all five mechanisms at a common (K,AUROC_0), a per-mechanism-intercept model beats a pooled one decisively (the R^2/LOO-R^2/F-test statistics and the 304x intercept spread are given once, in §5.9), and a pooled fit with no mechanism term gives a wrong-signed ln K coefficient that flips to correctly signed once mechanism is controlled for. Some factor beyond candidate count and the operating point is not absorbed by them; §5.9 states plainly this cannot yet fully separate mechanism identity from the unequal harness maturity/power correlated with it (Mechanism 3 underpowered, Mechanism 2 newly built but now checked against an independently-built second construction and found stable — one robustness check on one of the two least-mature harnesses, not a full resolution). This is a controlled-harness finding across five reconstructions of unequal maturity, not a claim about the five real audited pipelines at a matched operating point — §5.4 already shows that transport question fails by an order of magnitude — but within its own scope it narrows this line of work's single highest-value open question.

The difficulty of establishing any of these numbers — needing calibration, budget-matching, seed-matching, an alternative-confound test and a leak-free measurement instrument all to hold at once, and in Mechanism 3's case, a headline that dissolved when they did not — is this paper's strongest argument for the checklist we provide, more so than any single mechanism in isolation. Further robustness checks close specific gaps without moving any headline severity (§5.3, §, §): the fully-corrected Mechanism-3 estimator is itself calibrated rather than biased toward zero; decoupling candidate count from training length leaves the K-regularity intact; the checklist's prescribed fixes for Mechanisms 2 and 3 are validated against their naive alternatives at a cost of minutes of CPU time; a real-feature check for Mechanism 1 and a second model family for Mechanism 3 are both consistent with §5's operating-point account. Two checks complicate rather than confirm: Mechanisms 3 and 5 combine additively when both are present at once (§), but the operating-point decline is not strictly monotone under a non-Gaussian generative process or, at this seed count, even the paper's own Gaussian one (§), and the Dwork et al. adaptive-data-analysis bound exceeds every measured severity here by two to three orders of magnitude (§) — confirming it is not a usable auditing instrument at this paper's scale rather than a competing prediction. That checklist lets researchers and reviewers ask precise, mechanism-specific questions of a pipeline's evaluation protocol, replacing "did you use cross-validation correctly" as a single, undifferentiated concern. A pre-registered audit applying it to a fixed sample of 15-20 externally published probing papers, selected before any of them are examined, is planned as follow-on work; that protocol is already drafted.

## Appendix B. Extended Detail for Case Study 4

### B.1 Both estimators' non-negativity: statements, proofs, and degeneracies

§4.4 states both theorems and their consequences; the proofs are here. Let AinmathbbR^Lx R collect the AUROC of L probed layers under R seeds, with entries a_r,l. Write m(l)=tfrac1Rsum_r a_r,l for the grand mean at layer l and \[ f(r,l) := m(l)-a_{r,l}, \qquad\text{so that}\qquad \sum_{r} f(r,l)=0 \quad\text{for every fixed } l, \] an identity holding for any data. In rotation r the selection set is the R-1 seeds other than r, so the selection criterion at layer l is c_r(l)=fracsum_sneq r a_s,lR-1=m(l)+fracf(r,l)R-1, the rotation selects L_r=argmax_l c_r(l), and its winner's-curse term is wc_r=c_r(L_r)-a_r,L_r=f(r,L_r)*tfracRR-1. Averaging over rotations, \begin{equation} \Delta_{\text{wc}} = \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r}\text{wc}_r = \frac{1}{R-1}\sum_{r} f(r,L_r). \end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Non-negativity of the rotation estimator] Delta_wcge 0 for every AinmathbbR^Lx R, Rge2. \end{theorem}

\begin{proof} Let L^*inargmax_l m(l). Since L_r maximizes c_r, we have c_r(L_r)ge c_r(L^*), i.e. m(L_r)+tfracf(r,L_r)R-1ge m(L^*)+tfracf(r,L^*)R-1, which rearranges to f(r,L_r)ge (R-1)bigl(m(L^*)-m(L_r)bigr)+f(r,L^*). Summing over r and using sum_r f(r,L^*)=0, sum_r f(r,L_r)ge(R-1)sum_rbigl(m(L^*)-m(L_r)bigr)ge 0, each summand on the right being non-negative because L^* maximizes m. By~\eqref{eq:wc-reduced}, Delta_wcge0. \end{proof}

The equal-argmax degeneracy is the equality case: if L_r=L for all r then~\eqref{eq:wc-reduced} gives Delta_wc=tfrac1R-1sum_r f(r,L)=0 exactly. Writing a_0,a_1,a_2 for the three seeds' AUROC at that common layer and bar a for their mean, wc_r=tfrac32(bar a - a_r), pure noise summing to zero by construction: in those 7 of 24 cells the estimator is not measuring a small effect, it is measuring nothing. `code/59` verifies the theorem numerically over 300,000 random matrices from five families (uniform, Gaussian at scales 10^-6 to 10^6, clipped Cauchy, integer-discretized to manufacture ties, and AUROC-like values rounded to three decimals), sweeping Lin[2,40] and Rin[2,8); the most negative value observed is -2.3x10^-10, float summation noise around an algebraic zero.

**The replacement estimator is non-negative as well**, by Jensen's inequality rather than by cancellation. Write bar m(l)=tfrac1Rsum_r a_r,l, let c^*(l) be the mean at layer l over a uniform bootstrap resample of the R seed indices, and let L^*=argmax_l c^*(l). The estimator `code/45` implements is \begin{equation} \Delta_{\text{boot}} := \mathbb{E}^{*}\!\left[ c^{*}(L^{*})-\bar m(L^{*}) \right] = \mathbb{E}^{*}\!\left[\max_l c^{*}(l)\right]-\mathbb{E}^{*}\!\left[\bar m(L^{*})\right]. \end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Non-negativity of the bootstrap max-bias estimator] Delta_bootge0 for every AinmathbbR^Lx R, Rge1, with equality if and only if almost every resample's argmax is also a grand-mean argmax and conversely — generically, if and only if the grand-mean argmax is bootstrap-stable. \end{theorem}

\begin{proof} A uniform bootstrap resample of the seed indices is mean-preserving, so for each *fixed* l, mathbbE^*[c^*(l)]=bar m(l). The maximum is a convex functional of (c^*(l))_l, so by Jensen's inequality mathbbE^*[max_l c^*(l)]gemax_l mathbbE^*[c^*(l)] =max_l bar m(l). Separately, bar m(L^*)lemax_l bar m(l) for every realized L^*, so mathbbE^*[bar m(L^*)]lemax_l bar m(l). Substituting both into~\eqref{eq:boot-split} gives Delta_bootgemax_lbar m(l)-max_lbar m(l)=0. Equality forces both bounds to be tight simultaneously, which is the stated condition. \end{proof}

`code/70` verifies this over 50,000 random matrices at R=3 and 2,500 at R=5, by exact enumeration rather than sampling; the most negative value observed is -1.6x10^-14, again float residue. **The two cells an earlier revision reported as negative were Monte-Carlo noise.** With R=3 there are exactly 3^3=27 equiprobable resamples and Delta_boot has a closed form; enumerating all 27, `llama3.1-8b/truthfulqa/linear` and `qwen2.5-7b/truthfulqa/linear` are -1.1x10^-16 and +3.3x10^-17 rather than -0.0001 and -0.0007. They are precisely the two cells whose grand-mean argmax is bootstrap-stable, i.e. the equality case of Theorem~. The largest per-cell Monte-Carlo error was 6.8x10^-4, comparable to a third of the pooled estimate; the superseded values are retained in the shipped JSON as `bootstrap_max_bias_monte_carlo_legacy`.

**Which quantity is actually being computed.** `code/45`'s implementation is *not* the textbook bootstrap bias of a max-of-means, which subtracts the full-sample mean at the *full-sample* argmax, Delta_boot^std:=mathbbE^*[max_l c^*(l)]-max_l bar m(l), whereas the implemented one subtracts it at the *resample-selected* layer. Step (i) of the proof alone gives Delta_boot^stdge0, and Delta_boot-Delta_boot^std=max_lbar m(l)-mathbbE^*[bar m(L^*)]ge0, so the implemented version is always the larger. On these 24 cells it is 2.35x larger: +0.0021 against +0.0009 (BCa 95% CI [+0.0004,+0.0022]), with 6 of 24 cells exactly zero under the standard version against 2 under the implemented one. Both are reported; \S is what decides between them, on calibration rather than on which is larger. **Power, labelled by estimator:** under Delta_boot the 24 per-cell values have SD 0.0027 and the pooled design's minimum detectable effect at alpha=0.05, 80% power is +0.0015; under Delta_wc, SD 0.0079 and +0.0045. Neither should be read as a detectability claim about the *true* bias, since \S shows both estimators are biased by more than this dispersion.

### B.2 Calibrating the estimators against a known ground truth

**Design.** The audited repository selects hat L=argmax_l bar a(l) over the R=3 seeds and reports bar a(hat L), so the quantity a reader is misled by is bias_true=mathbbE[bar a(hat L)-mu(hat L)]. `code/71` generates data from the two-way model a_r,l=mu(l)+s_r+varepsilon_r,l at L=33, R=3. The seed main effect s_r is large in these files but constant in l, so it neither moves the argmax nor survives in expectation; only the layer x seed interaction varepsilon drives a winner's curse, and its SD is estimated by the two-way residual, median 0.0211 across the 24 real cells (1.29x smaller than the pooled within-layer SD, which conflates the two). bias_true is computed by 400,000-draw Monte Carlo independently of any estimator, and every estimator by exact enumeration of the 27 resamples. **The informative null is a known zero with noise still present**: holding the noise fixed and separating one layer by sep*sigma drives hat L deterministic and bias_true->0. At sep=16, bias_true=-1.0x10^-6 and all three estimators return 0 to float precision, with not one negative value in 20,000 simulated datasets — the behaviour Theorems~ and~ predict, now observed rather than only proved.

**The correction does not depend on identifying the data-generating condition.** An i.i.d.-interaction model fitted cell by cell produces a winner's curse about 4.6x larger than observed, because a seed's deviation is correlated across adjacent layers, so the effective number of independent candidates is far below 33; sweeping an AR(1) correlation rho along the layer axis reproduces the observed +0.0021 at rho~0.98, above the measured median lag-1 residual autocorrelation of +0.71. Applying each estimator's own ratio to its own observed value gives +0.0039 (Delta_boot), +0.0030 (Delta_boot^std) and +0.0040 (Delta_wc): three structurally different estimators, corrected by three independently measured factors, agreeing to within +/-15% where their raw values span a factor of six. `code/81`'s joint (rho,sep) sweep then identifies the condition directly (§4.4). ### B.4 Is the 0.975 ceiling-saturation cutpoint an artifact? A sweep and a cutpoint-free regression

The 0.975 cutpoint splitting the 24 cells into ceiling-saturated and non-saturated subsets (\S5.6, "+0.0016 against +0.0026", a 1.6x ratio) is one specific threshold choice, and an exact 12/12 split from an arbitrarily-chosen cutpoint invites the question of whether the split is a property of the data or an artifact of that one number. `code/95` answers this two ways, pure post-hoc analysis of the already-collected 24 cells with zero new compute.

**Sweeping the cutpoint.** Over 0.95,0.96,0.97,0.975,0.98,0.99, the split is (16,8) at 0.95 and **exactly (12,12) at every one of the remaining five cutpoints**, with the non-saturated/saturated ratio moving only from 1.64x to 1.76x across the entire grid. **The 12/12 split is therefore not a fragile artifact of the one cutpoint reported in \S5.6**; it is stable across a wide neighbourhood of cutpoints, which is the opposite of what an arbitrary-threshold worry predicts and is itself worth reporting plainly.

**The cutpoint-free alternative** — regressing Delta_boot continuously against the selected layer's own naive selection AUROC across all 24 cells, no cutpoint at all — is more revealing, but only once one choice already established elsewhere in this paper is respected. Over all 24 cells the association is weak and not significant (Pearson r=-0.141, p=0.51); restricted to the 17 estimator-non-degenerate cells — \S's own exclusion criterion for the headline mean, since a degenerate cell's rotation sibling is algebraically forced regardless of the true effect — the association is negative and clears conventional significance (Pearson r=-0.664, p=0.0037; OLS R^2=0.44). **The underlying relationship between operating point and winner's-curse magnitude is therefore real and continuous, not an artifact of the binary split**, but it is only visible once the same degeneracy exclusion this paper already applies elsewhere is applied here too; pooling degenerate cells in with the rest (as a reader unfamiliar with \S's distinction might) washes the signal out entirely, which is itself a caution about applying this paper's per-cell statistics without its accompanying exclusion criteria.
